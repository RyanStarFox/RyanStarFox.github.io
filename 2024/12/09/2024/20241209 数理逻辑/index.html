数理逻辑

¶第一章 命题逻辑的基本概念

  1. 命题是一个陈述句，不能是悖论，有固定的真假值（只是这个值可以未知）
  2. $P\to Q= \neg P\vee Q$
  3. $P \leftrightarrow Q=(P\to Q)\wedge(Q\to P)$
  4. 合式公式
    1. 简单命题是合式公式
    2. 如果$A$是合式公式，那么$\neg A$也是合式公式
    3. 如果$A,B$是合式公式，那么$(A\vee B),(A\wedge B),(A\to B),(A\leftrightarrow B)也是合式公式$
    4. 当且仅当使用有限次(1),(2),(3)所组成的符号串是合式公式
  5. 重言式=永真式，矛盾式=永假式

¶第二章 命题逻辑的等值与推理运算

  1. $A$与$B$真值表相同，则$A=B$或者$A\Leftrightarrow B$

  2. 运算规律：

    1. 双重否定律

      $\neg\neg P=P$

    2. 结合律

      $(P\wedge Q)\wedge R=P\wedge(Q\wedge R)$

      $(P\vee Q)\vee R=P\wedge(Q\vee R)$

      $(P\leftrightarrow Q)\leftrightarrow R=P\leftrightarrow(Q\leftrightarrow R)$

      $(P\to Q)\to R\neq P\to(Q\to R)$

    3. 交换律

      $P\wedge Q=Q\wedge P$

      $P\vee Q=Q\vee P$

      $P\leftrightarrow Q=Q\leftrightarrow P$

      $P\to Q\neq Q\to P$

    4. 分配律

      $P\wedge(Q\vee R)=(P\wedge Q)\vee(P\wedge R)$

      $P\vee(Q\wedge R)=(P\vee Q)\wedge(P\vee R)$

      $P\leftrightarrow (Q\leftrightarrow R)\neq (P\leftrightarrow Q)\leftrightarrow (P\leftrightarrow Q)$

      $P\to(Q\to R)=(P\to Q)\to(P\to R)$

    5. 恒等律

      $P\wedge P=P$

      $P\vee P=P$

      $P\leftrightarrow P=T$

      $P\to P=T$

    6. 吸收率

      $P\vee(P\wedge Q)=P$

      $P\wedge(P\vee Q)=P$

    7. 摩根律

      $\neg (P\vee Q)=\neg P\wedge\neg Q$

      $\neg (P\wedge Q)=\neg P\vee\neg Q$

      $\neg(P\leftrightarrow Q)=\neg P\leftrightarrow Q=P\leftrightarrow\neg Q=(\neg P\wedge Q)\vee(P\wedge\neq Q)$

      $\neg(P\to Q)=P\wedge\neg Q$

    8. 同一律

      $P\vee F=P$

      $P\wedge T=P$

      $T\leftrightarrow P=P$

      $T\to P=P$

      $P\to F=\neg P$

      $P\leftrightarrow F=\neg P$

    9. 零律

      $P\wedge F=F$

      $P\vee T=T$

      $P\to T=T$

      $F\to P=T$

    10. 补余律

      $P\wedge\neg P=F$

      $P\vee\neg P=T$

      $P\to\neg P=\neg P$

      $\neg P\to P=P$

      $P\leftrightarrow\neg P=F$

  3. 常用：

    1. $P\to Q= \neg P\vee Q$
    2. $P\to Q=\neg Q\to\neg P$
    3. $P\to(Q\to R)=(P\wedge Q)\to R$
    4. $P\leftrightarrow Q=(P\wedge Q)\vee(\neg P\wedge\neg Q)$
    5. $P\leftrightarrow Q=(P\vee\neg Q)\wedge(\neg P\vee Q)$
    6. $P\leftrightarrow Q=(P\to Q)\vee (Q\to P)$
    7. $P\to(Q\to R)=Q\to(P\to R)$
    8. $(P\to R)\wedge(Q\to R)=(P\vee Q)\to R$

  4. 永真式、永假式、可满足式

  5. $\bar\vee异或，\uparrow与非，\downarrow或非$

  6. 连接词的完备集：${\neg,\wedge,\vee},{\neg,\vee},{\neg,\wedge},{\neg,\to},{\uparrow},{\downarrow}$

  7. 对偶式：将$A$中的$\vee,\wedge,T,F$换成$\wedge,\vee,F,T$，记作$A^*$

  8. 若$A=A(P_1,P_2,\cdots,P_n)，$则令$A^-=A(\neg P_1,\neg P_2,\cdots,\neg P_n)$

  9. $\neg (A^)=(\neg A)^,\neg(A^-)=(\neg A)^-$

    $(A^)^=A,(A^-)^-=A$

    $\neg A = A^{*-}$

    $A=B \Leftrightarrow A^=B^$

    $A\to B与B^*\to A^*同永真$

    $A与A^-同永真性，\neg A与A^*同永真性$

  10. 析取范式形如$A_1\vee A_2\vee\cdots\vee A_n$

合取范式形如$A_1\wedge A_2\wedge\cdots\wedge A_n$

任一命题都有与之等值的合取范式和析取范式

求范式的步骤：

  1. 消去$\to, \leftrightarrow$

  2. 使用摩根律和双重否定律，把否定词移到命题变项上

  3. 重复使用分配律

  4. 仅由极小项构成的析取式为主析取范式，极小项用$m_i$表示

    仅由极大项构成的合取式为主合取范式，极大项用$M_i$表示

  5. 前提真结论必真的推理形式为正确的推理形式

  6. $(A\Rightarrow B)\Leftrightarrow(A\to B)$

    $(A\Rightarrow B)\Leftrightarrow (A\wedge \neg B)$

¶第三章 命题逻辑的公理化

  1. 公理系统：

    • 初始符号：公理系统允许出现的全体符号的集合
    • 形成规则：定义了合法序列
    • 公理：最基本的重言式
    • 变形规则
    • 定理：所有的重言式和它们的证明

  2. 命题逻辑的公理系统

    • 初始符号：
      • $A,B,C\cdots表示命题$
      • $\wedge,\vee,\neg表示连接词$
      • $( )圆括号$
      • $\vdash加在公式之前，表示其是永真式$
    • 形成规则：
      • $符号\pi是合式公式，\pi取值为A,B,\cdots$
      • $若A，B是合式公式，则(A\vee B)是合式公式$
      • $若A是合式公式，则\neg A是合式公式$
      • $只有符合(1),(2),(3)的符号序列才是合式公式$
    • 定义：
      • $(A\to B)定义为(\neg A\vee B)$
      • $(A\wedge B)定义为\neg(\neg A\vee\neg B)$
      • $(A\leftrightarrow B)定义为((A\to B)\wedge(B\to A))$
    • 公理：
      • $\vdash((P\vee P)\to P)$
      • $\vdash(P\to(P\vee Q))$
      • $\vdash ((P\vee Q)\to(Q\vee P))$
      • $\vdash((Q\to R)\to((P\vee Q)\to(P\vee R)))$
    • 推理规则
      • $代入原则$
      • $分离原则：\vdash A,\vdash A\to B\Rightarrow\vdash B$
      • $置换规则$

  3. 若所有成立的定理都可由系统推导出来，则称其为完备系统

  4. 推理方法：

    $\gamma\to\alpha\wedge \beta \Rightarrow (\gamma\to\alpha) \wedge(\gamma\to\beta)$

    $\gamma\to\alpha\vee \beta\Rightarrow\gamma\wedge \neg\alpha\to\beta$

    $\alpha\wedge\beta\to\gamma\Rightarrow\alpha\to(\beta\to\gamma)$

    $\alpha\vee\beta\to\gamma\Rightarrow(\alpha\to\gamma)\wedge(\beta\to\gamma)$

¶第四章 谓词逻辑的基本概念

  1. 受量词约束->约束变元；

    不受量词约束->自由变元

  2. 严格来说，x要在有限域内，$\forall x$和$\exists x$才能称为合式公式

¶第五章 谓词逻辑的等值和推理演算

  1. 等值式

    $\neg (\forall x)P(x)=(\exists x)\neg P(x)$

    $\neg (\exists x)P(X)=(\forall x)\neg P(x)$

    $(\forall x)(P(x)\to q)=(\exists x)P(x)\to q$

    $(\exists x)(P(x)\to q)=(\forall x)P(x)\to q$

    $(\forall x)(p\to Q(x))=p\to(\forall x)Q(x)$

    $(\exists x)(p\to Q(x))=p\to(\exists x)Q(x)$

    $(\forall x)(P(x)\wedge Q(x))=(\forall x)P(x)\wedge (\forall x)Q(x)$

    $(\exists x)(P(x)\vee Q(X))=(\exists x)P(x)\vee(\exists x)Q(x)$

    $(\forall x)(\forall y)(P(x)\vee Q(y))=(\forall x)P(x)\vee(\forall x)Q(x)$

    $(\exists x)(\exists y)(P(x)\wedge Q(y))=(\exists x)P(x)\wedge(\exists x)Q(x)$

  2. 范式

    1. 前束范式

      $A=(Q_1x_1)\cdots(Q_nx_n)M(x_1,\cdots,x_n)$

      $Q_i为量词，M称作公式A的母式（基式），M中不再有量词$

    2. Skolem标准形

      $(\exists x_1)\cdots(\exists x_i)(\forall x_{i+1})\cdots(\forall x_n)M(x_1,\cdots,x_n)$

  3. 基本的推理公式

    1. $(\forall x) P(x)\vee(\forall x)Q(x)\Rightarrow (\forall x)(P(x)\vee Q(x))$
    2. $(\exists x)(P(x)\wedge Q(x))\Rightarrow (\exists x)P(x)\wedge (\exists x)Q(x)$
    3. $(\forall x)(P(x)\to Q(x))\Rightarrow(\forall x)P(x)\to(\forall x)Q(x)$
    4. $(\forall x)(P(x)\to Q(x))\Rightarrow(\exists x)P(x)\to(\exists x)Q(x)$
    5. $(\forall x)(P(x)\leftrightarrow Q(x))\Rightarrow(\forall x)P(x)\leftrightarrow(\forall x)Q(x)$
    6. $(\forall x)(P(x)\leftrightarrow Q(x))\Rightarrow(\exists x)P(x)\leftrightarrow(\exists x)Q(x)$
    7. $(\forall x)(P(x)\to Q(x))\wedge(\forall x)(Q(x)\to R(X))\Rightarrow(\forall x)(P(x)\to R(X))$
    8. $(\forall x)(P(X)\to Q(X))\wedge P(a)\Rightarrow Q(a)$
    9. $(\forall x)(\forall y)P(x,y)\Rightarrow (\exists x)(\forall y)P(x,y)$
    10. $(\exists x)(\forall y)P(x,y)\Rightarrow(\forall y)(\exists x)P(x,y)$

Mathematical Logic

¶Chapter 1: Basic Concepts of Propositional Logic

  1. A proposition is a declarative sentence; it cannot be a paradox and has a fixed truth value (even if this value is unknown).
  2. $P\to Q= \neg P\vee Q$
  3. $P \leftrightarrow Q=(P\to Q)\wedge(Q\to P)$
  4. Well-formed formulas:
    1. Simple propositions are well-formed formulas.
    2. If $A$ is a well-formed formula, then $\neg A$ is also a well-formed formula.
    3. If $A$ and $B$ are well-formed formulas, then $(A\vee B)$, $(A\wedge B)$, $(A\to B)$, and $(A\leftrightarrow B)$ are also well-formed formulas.
    4. A string of symbols is a well-formed formula if and only if it is formed using (1), (2), and (3) a finite number of times.
  5. A tautology is equivalent to a universally true formula; a contradiction is equivalent to a universally false formula.

¶Chapter 2: Equivalences and Inference Operations in Propositional Logic

  1. If the truth tables of $A$ and $B$ are identical, then $A=B$ or $A\Leftrightarrow B$.

  2. Operational rules:

    1. Double negation law:

      $\neg\neg P=P$

    2. Associativity law:

      $(P\wedge Q)\wedge R=P\wedge(Q\wedge R)$

      $(P\vee Q)\vee R=P\vee(Q\vee R)$

      $(P\leftrightarrow Q)\leftrightarrow R=P\leftrightarrow(Q\leftrightarrow R)$

      $(P\to Q)\to R\neq P\to(Q\to R)$

    3. Commutativity law:

      $P\wedge Q=Q\wedge P$

      $P\vee Q=Q\vee P$

      $P\leftrightarrow Q=Q\leftrightarrow P$

      $P\to Q\neq Q\to P$

    4. Distributivity law:

      $P\wedge(Q\vee R)=(P\wedge Q)\vee(P\wedge R)$

      $P\vee(Q\wedge R)=(P\vee Q)\wedge(P\vee R)$

      $P\leftrightarrow (Q\leftrightarrow R)\neq (P\leftrightarrow Q)\leftrightarrow (P\leftrightarrow Q)$

      $P\to(Q\to R)=(P\to Q)\to(P\to R)$

    5. Identity law:

      $P\wedge P=P$

      $P\vee P=P$

      $P\leftrightarrow P=T$

      $P\to P=T$

    6. Absorption law:

      $P\vee(P\wedge Q)=P$

      $P\wedge(P\vee Q)=P$

    7. De Morgan's laws:

      $\neg (P\vee Q)=\neg P\wedge\neg Q$

      $\neg (P\wedge Q)=\neg P\vee\neg Q$

      $\neg(P\leftrightarrow Q)=\neg P\leftrightarrow Q=P\leftrightarrow\neg Q=(\neg P\wedge Q)\vee(P\wedge\neg Q)$

      $\neg(P\to Q)=P\wedge\neg Q$

    8. Identity law:

      $P\vee F=P$

      $P\wedge T=P$

      $T\leftrightarrow P=P$

      $T\to P=P$

      $P\to F=\neg P$

      $P\leftrightarrow F=\neg P$

    9. Zero law:

      $P\wedge F=F$

      $P\vee T=T$

      $P\to T=T$

      $F\to P=T$

    10. Complementarity law:

      $P\wedge\neg P=F$

      $P\vee\neg P=T$

      $P\to\neg P=\neg P$

      $\neg P\to P=P$

      $P\leftrightarrow\neg P=F$

  3. Common equivalences:

    1. $P\to Q= \neg P\vee Q$
    2. $P\to Q=\neg Q\to\neg P$
    3. $P\to(Q\to R)=(P\wedge Q)\to R$
    4. $P\leftrightarrow Q=(P\wedge Q)\vee(\neg P\wedge\neg Q)$
    5. $P\leftrightarrow Q=(P\vee\neg Q)\wedge(\neg P\vee Q)$
    6. $P\leftrightarrow Q=(P\to Q)\vee (Q\to P)$
    7. $P\to(Q\to R)=Q\to(P\to R)$
    8. $(P\to R)\wedge(Q\to R)=(P\vee Q)\to R$

  4. Tautologies, contradictions, and satisfiable formulas.

  5. $\bar\vee$ represents XOR, $\uparrow$ represents NAND, and $\downarrow$ represents NOR.

  6. Complete sets of connectives: ${\neg,\wedge,\vee}$, ${\neg,\vee}$, ${\neg,\wedge}$, ${\neg,\to}$, ${\uparrow}$, ${\downarrow}$.

  7. Dual formula: Replace $\vee$, $\wedge$, $T$, and $F$ in $A$ with $\wedge$, $\vee$, $F$, and $T$ respectively; denote it as $A^*$.

  8. If $A=A(P_1,P_2,\cdots,P_n)$, then let $A^-=A(\neg P_1,\neg P_2,\cdots,\neg P_n)$.

  9. $\neg (A^)=(\neg A)^,\neg(A^-)=(\neg A)^-$

    $(A^)^=A,(A^-)^-=A$

    $\neg A = A^{*-}$

    $A=B \Leftrightarrow A^=B^$

    $A\to B$ and $B^\to A^$ have the same truth value.

    $A$ and $A^-$ have the same truth value; $\neg A$ and $A^*$ have the same truth value.

  10. Disjunctive normal form has the form $A_1\vee A_2\vee\cdots\vee A_n$.

    Conjunctive normal form has the form $A_1\wedge A_2\wedge\cdots\wedge A_n$.

    Every proposition has an equivalent conjunctive normal form and disjunctive normal form.

    Steps to obtain normal forms:

  11. Eliminate $\to$ and $\leftrightarrow$.

  12. Use De Morgan's laws and the double negation law to move negations onto propositional variables.

  13. Apply the distributive law repeatedly.

  14. A disjunction consisting only of minterms is the principal disjunctive normal form; minterms are denoted as $m_i$.

    A conjunction consisting only of maxterms is the principal conjunctive normal form; maxterms are denoted as $M_i$.

  15. An inference form where the conclusion is true whenever the premises are true is a valid inference form.

  16. $(A\Rightarrow B)\Leftrightarrow(A\to B)$

    $(A\Rightarrow B)\Leftrightarrow (A\wedge \neg B)$

¶Chapter 3: Axiomatization of Propositional Logic

  1. Axiomatic system:

    • Initial symbols: The set of all symbols allowed in the axiomatic system.
    • Formation rules: Define valid sequences.
    • Axioms: The most basic tautologies.
    • Transformation rules.
    • Theorems: All tautologies and their proofs.

  2. Axiomatic system for propositional logic:

    • Initial symbols:
      • $A,B,C\cdots$ represent propositions.
      • $\wedge$, $\vee$, $
        eg$ represent connectives.
      • Parentheses $()$.
      • $\vdash$ placed before a formula indicates it is a tautology.
    • Formation rules:
      • Symbol $\pi$ is a well-formed formula, where $\pi$ takes values $A,B,\cdots$.
      • If $A$ and $B$ are well-formed formulas, then $(A\vee B)$ is a well-formed formula.
      • If $A$ is a well-formed formula, then $\neg A$ is a well-formed formula.
      • Only symbol sequences that conform to (1), (2), and (3) are well-formed formulas.
    • Definitions:
      • $(A\to B)$ is defined as $(\neg A\vee B)$.
      • $(A\wedge B)$ is defined as $\neg(\neg A\vee\neg B)$.
      • $(A\leftrightarrow B)$ is defined as $((A\to B)\wedge(B\to A))$.
    • Axioms:
      • $\vdash((P\vee P)\to P)$
      • $\vdash(P\to(P\vee Q))$
      • $\vdash ((P\vee Q)\to(Q\vee P))$
      • $\vdash((Q\to R)\to((P\vee Q)\to(P\vee R)))$
    • Inference rules:
      • Substitution principle.
      • Modus ponens: $\vdash A,\vdash A\to B\Rightarrow\vdash B$.
      • Substitution rule.

  3. If all valid theorems can be derived from the system, it is called a complete system.

  4. Inference methods:

    $\gamma\to\alpha\wedge \beta \Rightarrow (\gamma\to\alpha) \wedge(\gamma\to\beta)$

    $\gamma\to\alpha\vee \beta\Rightarrow\gamma\wedge \neg\alpha\to\beta$

    $\alpha\wedge\beta\to\gamma\Rightarrow\alpha\to(\beta\to\gamma)$

    $\alpha\vee\beta\to\gamma\Rightarrow(\alpha\to\gamma)\wedge(\beta\to\gamma)$

¶Chapter 4: Basic Concepts of Predicate Logic

  1. Variables bound by quantifiers are called bound variables; variables not bound by quantifiers are called free variables.
  2. Strictly speaking, $x$ must belong to a finite domain for $\forall x$ and $\exists x$ to be considered well-formed formulas.

¶Chapter 5: Equivalences and Inference Calculations in Predicate Logic

  1. Equivalence formulas:

    $\neg (\forall x)P(x)=(\exists x)\neg P(x)$

    $\neg (\exists x)P(x)=(\forall x)\neg P(x)$

    $(\forall x)(P(x)\to q)=(\exists x)P(x)\to q$

    $(\exists x)(P(x)\to q)=(\forall x)P(x)\to q$

    $(\forall x)(p\to Q(x))=p\to(\forall x)Q(x)$

    $(\exists x)(p\to Q(x))=p\to(\exists x)Q(x)$

    $(\forall x)(P(x)\wedge Q(x))=(\forall x)P(x)\wedge (\forall x)Q(x)$

    $(\exists x)(P(x)\vee Q(x))=(\exists x)P(x)\vee(\exists x)Q(x)$

    $(\forall x)(\forall y)(P(x)\vee Q(y))=(\forall x)P(x)\vee(\forall x)Q(x)$

    $(\exists x)(\exists y)(P(x)\wedge Q(y))=(\exists x)P(x)\wedge(\exists x)Q(x)$

  2. Normal forms:

    1. Prenex normal form:

      $A=(Q_1x_1)\cdots(Q_nx_n)M(x_1,\cdots,x_n)$

      $Q_i$ are quantifiers; $M$ is called the matrix (or base formula) of formula $A$, and there are no quantifiers in $M$.

    2. Skolem normal form:

      $(\exists x_1)\cdots(\exists x_i)(\forall x_{i+1})\cdots(\forall x_n)M(x_1,\cdots,x_n)$

  3. Basic inference formulas:

    1. $(\forall x) P(x)\vee(\forall x)Q(x)\Rightarrow (\forall x)(P(x)\vee Q(x))$
    2. $(\exists x)(P(x)\wedge Q(x))\Rightarrow (\exists x)P(x)\wedge (\exists x)Q(x)$
    3. $(\forall x)(P(x)\to Q(x))\Rightarrow(\forall x)P(x)\to(\forall x)Q(x)$
    4. $(\forall x)(P(x)\to Q(x))\Rightarrow(\exists x)P(x)\to(\exists x)Q(x)$
    5. $(\forall x)(P(x)\leftrightarrow Q(x))\Rightarrow(\forall x)P(x)\leftrightarrow(\forall x)Q(x)$
    6. $(\forall x)(P(x)\leftrightarrow Q(x))\Rightarrow(\exists x)P(x)\leftrightarrow(\exists x)Q(x)$
    7. $(\forall x)(P(x)\to Q(x))\wedge(\forall x)(Q(x)\to R(x))\Rightarrow(\forall x)(P(x)\to R(x))$
    8. $(\forall x)(P(x)\to Q(x))\wedge P(a)\Rightarrow Q(a)$
    9. $(\forall x)(\forall y)P(x,y)\Rightarrow (\exists x)(\forall y)P(x,y)$
    10. $(\exists x)(\forall y)P(x,y)\Rightarrow(\forall y)(\exists x)P(x,y)$
