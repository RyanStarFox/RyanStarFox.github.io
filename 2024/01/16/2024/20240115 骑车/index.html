Ride, ride through the day

我能接受的最快的还算能享受的速度是自行车的速度。

在读大学以前，我很少骑车。出行无非三种方式：步行、乘父母的车、乘公交。乘公交的时光是漫长的，总需要一样东西来分散注意力，打磨时光。对我而言，那常常是手中的Kindle和耳朵里的AirPods。公交车是颠簸的，有一股怪味，窗缝和座位底下总感觉很脏，车门打开时候嘎吱嘎吱的。地铁稍微稳一点，但是隆隆的运行声让我总要带上降噪耳机，即使我不想听音乐。在公交上，我能感受到阅读和欣赏音乐的快乐，但是我不享受这段单调、重复、死板的旅程。

乘父母的车比乘公交舒服一些。至少有一整面的窗户是留给我的，我可以看看路边的景色。可这景色往往是单调的——在这个车速限制的观察的宏观尺度上、在城市里，我看到的几乎只有千篇一律的楼和不断重复的路灯、路牌、和路上的标线。对我而言，享受的纯粹的车程可能只有那种空旷、笔直、怪石环绕的无人公路或是溪边的人小路。感觉山石和水流是少数不多在宏观的尺度之下还能欣赏的东西了。平时乘车唯一的享受之处在于与家人的聊天，但这也不属于旅途的享受的范畴了。

但是每次步行就很享受。步行足够慢，慢到我可以看清路途中的一切细节；步行足够随意，我想停便停、想快便快、想慢便慢；步行足够放松，我可以肆意挥霍自己的注意力；步行足够真实，我可以去触碰大地，听落叶的声音，嗅即将下雨的味道。步行是生活；步行是探索；步行是欣赏；步行是作诗。

高中毕业之后，第一个新的交通体验是亲自开车。我的母亲很惊讶我没有像刻板印象中的男孩一样喜欢车这类所谓“阳刚”的东西。我没有很喜欢开车的感觉。我认为开车的感觉甚至比乘车还糟糕。除了不能好好看看身边的景，甚至注意力都被分开了——“看”就几乎只够实现最功利的目的了。

第二个新的交通体验是自行车。我上大学前自行车骑得不多，高中毕业后用从自己成功从SHS骑共享单车骑40km回家的经历换来了一辆入门的山地车giant-atx720。大学这一个学期，我骑的里程可能比我人生的前18年加起来都要多。我很享受骑行。骑行其实和步行很像——过了两三天后也还记得很多那段路的细节。在闵行骑车时每次都是绿灯，出了闵行就一直等红灯。过了一座桥后远远看到一座寺庙的大雄宝殿，在地图上查看发现是法华禅寺。非机动车道上奔波的除了疾驶的外卖小哥还有从工具箱里掉了一只扳手出来的维修工人。电瓶车的侧面伸出一个毛茸茸的头和一条兴奋地晃着的小尾巴，在等红绿灯时，那只小狗跳下了车，车上的老爷爷和隔壁车的老奶奶告诉它还没到，回去，它就回去了。看到嘉定新城一片风景秀丽的河边步道，旁边是红色的黄色的树，那是在嘉定城市规划馆旁边。馆对面还有一家养乐多工厂，以前我的太外婆经常送给我养乐多，一板一板的。骑着骑着，耳机里随机到了Queen的《Bicycle Race》。在骑过苏州河的时候，那个地方离虹桥很近，看到了4，5架飞的很低的飞机，就直直地在头顶掠过，连航空公司的名字都能看到。有幸拍下了几张照片，再往前骑一点是一帮在等着看飞机降落的年轻人。

很喜欢这张

《中国东方航空》

但是我最近意识到骑行的享受较步行已经有所下降了。我是在一天晚上意识到这件事的。那天晚上图书馆闭馆，和一个偶然遇到的高中同学一起回去。她没找到共享单车，所以我们一同走回去。在教超旁边，我看到一颗树，那棵树很粗，看上去年龄比旁边的行道树大了不少。一般而言，人感知到一件事往往能够快速唤醒一个人沉睡的回忆的。对我而言，最灵敏的是嗅觉，但是嗅觉稍纵即逝，往往我还没有想起那一段回忆就消逝了，空留感受的余音和美丽的遗憾。但是视觉是真切的，那棵粗得不一般的树让我立刻想到了小学上学路上和祖父一同依据树干粗细“钦定”的树老大、老二、老三和老四。我骑行的时候从未注意过这棵树。

步行、骑行。对于很多人来说，通勤是种折磨。是啊，谁愿意一大早起来挤在狭小的车厢内呢？步行作为通勤的方式可能在现在已经不太实际了，但是骑行还是可以作为一种折中的办法吧？我不愿意每一段路程都要想办法消磨时间，我还是希望多几段可以享受的旅程的。

Ride, ride through the day

The fastest speed I can accept and still enjoy is the speed of a bicycle.

Before college, I rarely rode a bike. My travel options were limited to three: walking, taking my parents’ car, or riding the bus. Bus rides are long, so I always needed something to distract me and pass the time. For me, that was often my Kindle in hand and AirPods in my ears. Buses are bumpy, have a strange smell, and the gaps in the windows and under the seats always feel dirty; the doors make a creaking sound when they open. The subway is a bit smoother, but its rumbling noise forces me to wear noise-canceling headphones even when I don’t want to listen to music. On the bus, I can enjoy the pleasure of reading and listening to music, but I don’t relish this monotonous, repetitive, and rigid journey.

Taking my parents’ car is more comfortable than taking the bus. At least I have an entire window to myself to admire the scenery along the road. But this scenery is often dull — at the macroscopic scale allowed by speed limits in the city, all I see are identical buildings, repeating streetlights, signs, and road markings. For me, the only truly enjoyable rides are on empty, straight country roads surrounded by strange rocks or narrow paths beside streams. It seems that mountains, rocks, and flowing water are among the few things still worth appreciating at a macroscopic level. The only enjoyable part of riding in a car is chatting with family, but that doesn’t count as part of the journey’s enjoyment.

But I always enjoy walking. Walking is slow enough for me to notice every detail along the way; it’s flexible enough for me to stop when I want, go fast or slow as I please; it’s relaxing enough for me to freely allocate my attention; and it’s real enough for me to touch the ground, hear the sound of falling leaves, and smell the scent of impending rain. Walking is life; walking is exploration; walking is appreciation; walking is poetry.

After graduating from high school, my first new transportation experience was driving a car myself. My mother was surprised that I didn’t like cars, those so-called “masculine” objects that boys in stereotypes are supposed to love. I don’t particularly enjoy driving; in fact, I find it even worse than being a passenger. Not only can’t I properly enjoy the scenery around me, but my attention is also distracted — “seeing” is reduced to fulfilling purely utilitarian purposes.

My second new transportation experience was riding a bicycle. I didn’t ride a bike much before college. After graduating from high school, I earned an entry-level mountain bike, a Giant ATX720, by successfully riding a shared bike 40km home from SHS. This semester in college, the distance I’ve ridden may exceed the total I covered in the first 18 years of my life. I really enjoy cycling. Cycling is actually quite similar to walking — I still remember many details of a route days after riding it. When riding in Minhang, I always hit green lights; once I leave Minhang, I keep waiting for red lights. After crossing a bridge, I saw the main hall of a temple in the distance; checking on the map, I found it was Fahua Zen Temple. Besides delivery riders speeding by, the non-motorized vehicle lane also has repair workers who drop a wrench out of their toolboxes. A fluffy head and an excited wagging tail stick out from the side of an electric scooter; while waiting for the light to turn green, the puppy jumps off the scooter, and the old man on the scooter and the old lady in the next vehicle tell it to go back since it’s not time yet, and it obeys. I saw a scenic riverside path in Jiading New City, with red and yellow trees nearby; it’s next to the Jiading Urban Planning Museum. Across from the museum, there’s also a Yakult factory; my great-grandmother used to give me Yakult in packs. As I rode along, my earbuds randomly played Queen’s Bicycle Race. When crossing the Suzhou River, a place close to Hongqiao, I saw 4 or 5 planes flying very low overhead, so close that I could even read the airline name. I was lucky enough to take a few photos; a little further ahead, there was a group of young people waiting to watch the planes land.

I really like this one.

China Eastern Airlines

However, I recently realized that the joy I get from cycling has declined compared to walking. I realized this one evening. That night, the library closed, and I went back with a high school classmate I ran into by chance. She couldn’t find a shared bike, so we walked back together. Next to the convenience store, I saw a tree — it was very thick and looked much older than the other street trees around it. Generally, perceiving something can quickly awaken dormant memories. For me, the most sensitive sense is smell, but it fades quickly; often, the memory disappears before I can recall it, leaving only a lingering feeling and a beautiful regret. But vision is concrete: that unusually thick tree immediately made me think of the “top four trees” I and my grandfather designated based on trunk thickness on my way to primary school. I never noticed this tree while cycling.

Walking and cycling. For many people, commuting is a torment. Indeed, who wants to wake up early and squeeze into a cramped carriage? Walking may no longer be a practical commuting option today, but cycling could serve as a compromise. I don’t want to spend every journey trying to kill time; I still hope for more journeys I can truly enjoy.
