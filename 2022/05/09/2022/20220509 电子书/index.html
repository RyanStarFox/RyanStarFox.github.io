漫谈电子书

第一篇知乎长文，聊聊我以前折腾过的电子书。

本人kindle voyage重度用户

格式

之前我有朋友问我电子书这么多格式有什么区别，我以前的瞎折腾结果和理解如下：

  1. pdf、docx、txt不是好格式！！！

  2. Epub：其为市面上最通用的格式，有广泛支持，但kindle不支持。（亚马逊嫌弃Epub更新太慢，自己过于先进，因此自己创造自由格式）

  3. azw3：亚马逊格式，把任何格式的书传到kindle云端，再从kindle下载，这本书的格式就会被自动转换为了azw3。在kindle上可以更换自己喜欢的字体，但不支持直接上传kindle云端，只能用USB拷到kindle里。

  4. Mobi：分为两类

    • 旧版的kf-7，支持上传kindle云端，兼容性更好，但不支持使用自己的字体。网上大多数.mobi文件都是kf-7，据说可以通过calibre把kf-7转换为kf-8，亲测无用，我的方法是先转换为epub，再用kindle官方的kindle previewer转换为kf-8。
    • 新版kf-8，支持上传kindle云端。支持使用自己的字体。是的，为了好看的字体，我愿意折腾。“自己的字体”指的是我自己拷到电脑的个性化字体，而不是自带的。自带的字体无论什么格式都可以自由转换。（azw3似乎也算一种kf8）

  5. Kfx：这个网上应该基本找不到吧？这是亚马逊官方的kindle previewer的内部格式，没啥用，比其他格式用kindle previewer打开更快一些。

  6. fb2：似乎是俄罗斯的一种格式？没有用过，不通用。

如果你有kindle，追求最佳的阅读体验——直接买kindle商店里的电子书。

如果你有kindle，追求最佳阅读体验，但是不想付钱——下载最大的epub格式电子书(可能包含更多图像)，再通过kindle previewer转换为kf-8 mobi。(有时，下载的epub不符合规范协议，无法转换，那么就换几个epub多试试。）

如果你没有kindle，kf8不是很有必要，几个格式都可。

apple自带的图书，据说对epub的规范性要求很高，可能需要试很多epub

有的epub是pdf直接转换来的，格式不规范显得凌乱（有很多奇怪的换行以及突然出现的数字（页码？）），要留个心眼。

A Casual Talk about E-books

This is my first long-form Zhihu post, sharing my past experiences with e-books.

I’m a heavy user of the Kindle Voyage.

Formats

A friend once asked me about the differences between various e-book formats, and here’s what I learned from my past experiments and understanding:

  1. PDF, DOCX, and TXT are not ideal formats!!!

  2. EPUB: It’s the most widely supported format on the market, but Kindle doesn’t support it. (Amazon dislikes EPUB for being too slow to update and considers it outdated compared to their own advanced formats, so they created their own proprietary format.)

  3. AZW3: This is Amazon’s format. When you upload any book format to the Kindle cloud and then download it to your Kindle, it will be automatically converted to AZW3. You can change the font you like on Kindle, but you can’t upload books directly to the Kindle cloud; you have to copy them to Kindle via USB.

  4. MOBI: There are two types:

    • The older KF-7 version supports uploading to the Kindle cloud and has better compatibility, but it doesn’t support custom fonts. Most .mobi files online are KF-7. I heard that KF-7 can be converted to KF-8 using Calibre, but I found it useless. My method is to first convert it to EPUB, then use Amazon’s official Kindle Previewer to convert it to KF-8.
    • The newer KF-8 version supports uploading to the Kindle cloud and allows the use of custom fonts. Yes, for the sake of nice-looking fonts, I’m willing to go through the trouble. “Custom fonts” refer to personalized fonts I copy to my computer, not the built-in ones. Built-in fonts can be freely converted regardless of the original format. (It seems AZW3 is also a type of KF-8.)

  5. KFX: You probably won’t find this format online. It’s an internal format used by Amazon’s official Kindle Previewer. It’s not very useful and only opens faster in Kindle Previewer than other formats.

  6. FB2: I think this is a Russian format? I’ve never used it, and it’s not very universal.

If you have a Kindle and want the best reading experience — just buy e-books from the Kindle store.

If you have a Kindle, want the best reading experience but don’t want to pay — download large EPUB-format e-books (which may contain more images), then use Kindle Previewer to convert them to KF-8 MOBI. (Sometimes, downloaded EPUBs do not comply with standard protocols and cannot be converted, so try a few different EPUBs.)

If you don’t have a Kindle, KF-8 isn’t very necessary; most formats work fine.

Apple’s built-in Books app reportedly has strict requirements for EPUB standards, so you might need to try many EPUBs.

Some EPUBs are directly converted from PDFs, resulting in messy formatting (with strange line breaks and randomly appearing numbers (page numbers?)). Keep this in mind.

Update:

Kindle has left China. I stayed loyal to it, but I couldn’t wait any longer. I bought a Boox Leaf 2 after the college entrance exam, so format is no longer a concern — almost all books can be downloaded in EPUB format.

Kindle really leaves me with mixed feelings, especially now: All I wanted was a Kindle with physical buttons and a Type-C port, but none has been released yet — I was expecting a Kindle Oasis with Type-C, but instead Amazon launched the Kindle Scribe and Kindle ColorSoft.

The Boox experience is great; although there are color deviation issues, they’re minor problems.
