在新国立做的梦

6/28晚上，我抵达新加坡国立大学，开始近一个月的暑期项目。现在我坐在uTown，不知道应该做什么。项目在今天下午完成了最后的展示，只要明天轮流坐在摊位前介绍就行了。展示之后，我不知道去哪里，想了想，还是来了uTown，一方面自己不习惯一直待在寝室里；另一方面其实是想吃潮州猪脚锅，吃一些猪肉。

我仍旧坐在Education Resources Center的那个靠近且面对大草坪的位置，但是今天却不知道该做些什么。天气还是很好，很多毕业生穿着学士服在拍毕业照，草地上几只小狗在玩闹，我旁边的一只哈士奇被大爷牵着也在朝那里看——这只哈士奇每天都来。我非常喜欢这里的云，这里的云是立体的，饱满的，竟让我感觉和西藏的云有点像，甚至同样都很低。云飘得很快，天渐渐暗下来，云变成橙色和黄色的了。我去Fine Food吃我心心念念的猪脚锅了。

我突然间意识到，这可能是我最后一次来uTown了，也可能是最后一次在uTown看夕阳了。我不知所措，是因为已经意识到这段旅程即将结束，我无法暂停时间，因而只想郑重地点上一个句点——这也让我猛然感到，应该写点东西了。

这是7年前的照片了

新加坡，我第一次来这里是7年之前。那个时候疫情尚未来临，父母还未离婚，经济仍然向好。那段旅程让我不敢忘记，因此我开始写日记，从2018年8月1日，也就是第一天从上海出发抵达新加坡的那一天开始。当时正好赶上了新加坡国庆，看到各种奇怪的飞机飞来飞去，回去的时候在樟宜机场一开始不愿四处逛，临走前又发现来不及去看看仙人掌馆。鸭子船、花穹、云雾岭、夜间动物园……我现在还能记起的似乎也只有这些毫无意义但是充满情感的碎片了——日记就是让我更加放心地遗忘吧。

这次出发前，还在做歌手大赛的那段时间，我就经常梦见自己来到新国立——梦中的新国立有一个类似上中的校门，门口就是地铁站和酒吧——我很向往，但是我不敢抱太高的期望，我担心仍是和一群没什么想法的理工男打交道。

新加坡的气候很好，几乎只有上午下过雨——那时候我还在睡觉，下午也没那么热，晚上开空调会冷——难怪这里有很多挂着大吊扇的开放空间。我十分羡慕新加坡的一点在于新加坡是能够在大楼里面种树的——花园城市名副其实。这里实际上碰到华人面孔都可以说中文——即使用蹩脚的Chinglish问，回答也很有可能是中文。新加坡真的很宜居，很想以后时不时来这里旅居。

在这里也趁空闲时间看了一些电影，包括飞机上看的《天堂电影院》、周末看的《卧虎藏龙》、《勇敢的心》、《低俗小说》、回家候机时看的《国产凌凌漆》，还看完了《混沌武士》。回想起来《混沌武士》中的三人和nus项目的小组很像——莫名甚至是非常虚的原因聚在一起，在晴朗的日子走了许多路，在皆大欢喜的结局后各自成长分别。闲时间做的最重要的事把《地频线》的决赛录像的录音提取了出来，做成了专辑自己听。于我，新加坡也有了《地频线》的影子。

我们的课程最终是需要分成小组来做project的模式。我们组基本上是大佬带飞的状态。最开始的时候，我称他们为组员，但是到了最后，我觉得可以称为朋友了——比我预想的好很多，他们目之所及不止眼前的电脑，而是有着对于世界的个人理解。我们一起做project，一起吃饭，一起去市区，一起看电影，一起庆祝一位的生日，甚至一起做了一件疯狂的事——新加坡骑行一圈60公里——骑到东海岸的路不太好骑，常常被红灯阻挠，但是一到东海岸就变得非常顺畅舒适——海边的音乐、椰子树的剪影、海滩黄昏与混杂着咸味和烧烤味的海风让我想起我小时候去普吉岛，也是热带的海滩的日落，海边的果树挂满了青色的芒果。樟宜机场来来往往的飞机从我们头顶飞过，时不时飞过一些为了国庆训练的军机。与其中一位有摩擦，但同样也碰到了只去相投的朋友。感觉在这里我彻底改去了对于电院全是没有想法的nerd的固有印象——其实还是有很多人闪烁着思想的光芒，他们不仅着眼于眼前的技术，也看见技术背后的目的。他们的技术观和世界观、人生观相互支撑，也已经到达相当深入的鲜有人涉足的地方了。其间，我意识到一个有趣的现象：其实有几位我甚至会把他们投射到一些故人上去——气质很像，样貌很像，但是又确确实实不是同一个人。

当然，我也故地重游。我到新加坡的第二天就去了滨海湾。一切如故，但人变了，世界也变了。让我想起《星际牛仔》里Faye见到夕阳下的鱼尾狮，向着家的方向跑去，但是只见断壁颓垣和那一首《Call me Call me》。云雾林和花穹也还是和原来差不多。云雾岭最近好像在和侏罗纪公园合作，可能是为最近的电影做宣发吧。我小时候在花穹的照片作为个人照，在很多地方使用过——荣誉册、精选PPT，我在同样的位置又拍了一张，但是现在那里全都是玫瑰而不是以前的黄橙色的花儿。曾经的我淹没在花丛中，但是现在花丛却是在脚边。到圣淘沙的时候正好夕阳西下，为了看夕阳，并没有去小时去过的亚洲最南端——不知道现在那里是不是还是那些栈道。我往西走，踩着柔软的沙子，看着紫红色的天水交接之处和停泊在岸边的货轮。

算是认真、郑重的画了个句号吧。天色已经完全暗下来，uTown优雅的灯光亮起来了。不知以后是否还有缘再来NUS，至于新加坡，想必以后常会再来吧？

A Dream at NUS

On the evening of June 28th, I arrived at the National University of Singapore to start a nearly one-month summer program. Right now, I’m sitting in uTown, unsure what to do. The final presentation for the program was completed this afternoon; all that’s left is to take turns manning the booth to introduce our work tomorrow. After the presentation, I didn’t know where to go, so I decided to come to uTown. On one hand, I’m not used to staying in my dormitory all the time; on the other hand, I really wanted to eat Teochew pork knuckle hotpot and have some pork.

I’m still sitting in the spot near and facing the lawn at the Education Resources Center, but today I have no idea what to do. The weather is still lovely: many graduates are wearing their graduation gowns to take photos, a few puppies are playing on the grass, and a Husky next to me, led by an old man, is also looking over there—this Husky comes here every day. I love the clouds here; they are three-dimensional and plump, making me feel they’re a bit like the clouds in Tibet, and they’re even low-lying too. The clouds drift quickly, and as it gets darker, they turn orange and yellow. I went to Fine Food to eat the pork knuckle hotpot I’d been craving.

I suddenly realized that this might be my last time visiting uTown, and possibly my last time watching the sunset here. I’m at a loss because I’ve realized this journey is coming to an end. I can’t pause time, so I just want to draw a solemn full stop—this also made me suddenly feel that I should write something down.

This photo was taken 7 years ago.

It’s been 7 years since I first came to Singapore. Back then, the pandemic hadn’t hit yet, my parents hadn’t divorced, and the economy was still doing well. I dare not forget that journey, so I started keeping a diary starting from August 1, 2018, the day I arrived in Singapore from Shanghai. I happened to catch Singapore’s National Day then; I saw all sorts of strange planes flying around. When I left, I didn’t want to wander around Changi Airport at first, but before departing I realized I didn’t have time to visit the Cactus House. Duck boats, Flower Dome, Cloud Forest, Night Safari… the only things I can still remember seem to be these meaningless but emotionally charged fragments—diaries let me forget more easily.

Before I set off, during the time I was participating in a singing competition, I often dreamed of coming to NUS—in my dream, NUS had a school gate similar to that of Shanghai High School, with a subway station and bars right at the entrance. I yearned for it, but I didn’t dare to set my hopes too high; I worried I’d still be dealing with a bunch of clueless engineering students.

Singapore has great weather; it only rained in the morning—when I was still sleeping—and it wasn’t that hot in the afternoon. It gets cold when the air conditioning is on at night—no wonder there are so many open spaces with large ceiling fans here. One thing I envy about Singapore is that trees can be planted inside buildings—the title of “Garden City” is well-deserved. In fact, you can speak Chinese with anyone of Chinese descent here; even if you ask in broken Chinglish, the reply is likely to be in Chinese. Singapore is really livable; I’d love to come here for a staycation from time to time in the future.

I also watched a few movies in my spare time here, including Cinema Paradiso on the plane, Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon, Braveheart, Pulp Fiction on the weekend, Shaolin Soccer while waiting to board my flight home, and I finished watching Samurai Champloo. Looking back, the trio in Samurai Champloo is very similar to my group in the NUS program—they came together for inexplicable, almost trivial reasons, walked a lot on sunny days, and grew and parted ways after a happy ending. The most important thing I did in my spare time was extract the audio from the finals recording of DiPinXian and make an album for myself to listen to. For me, Singapore now also has a trace of DiPinXian.

Our course ultimately required us to work on projects in groups. My group was basically led by top performers. At first, I called them group members, but by the end, I felt we could call each other friends—it turned out much better than I expected. Their vision wasn’t limited to the computer in front of them; they had their own understanding of the world. We worked on projects together, ate together, went to the city together, watched movies together, celebrated a teammate’s birthday together, and even did a crazy thing together—riding 60 kilometers around Singapore. The road to East Coast wasn’t easy to ride on; we were often held up by red lights, but once we reached East Coast, it became very smooth and comfortable. The music by the sea, the silhouettes of coconut trees, the beach sunset, and the sea breeze mixed with the smell of salt and barbecue reminded me of my childhood trips to Phuket—those tropical beach sunsets with green mangoes hanging on the seaside trees. Planes coming and going at Changi Airport flew over our heads, and from time to time, military planes training for National Day flew by. I had a few conflicts with one person, but I also met like-minded friends. I feel I’ve completely changed my preconceived notion that all students from the electrical engineering department are clueless nerds—in fact, there are many people whose minds shine brightly; they don’t just focus on current technologies but also see the purposes behind them. Their views on technology, the world, and life support each other, and they’ve reached rather profound realms that few others explore. During this time, I noticed an interesting phenomenon: there are a few people I even project former acquaintances onto—they have similar temperaments and appearances, but they’re definitely not the same people.

Of course, I also revisited old places. I went to Marina Bay the day after I arrived in Singapore. Everything was the same, but the people and the world had changed. It reminded me of Faye in Cowboy Bebop seeing the Merlion under the sunset and running towards home, only to find ruins and the song Call Me Call Me. The Cloud Forest and Flower Dome are still pretty much the same as before. The Cloud Forest seems to be collaborating with Jurassic Park recently, probably for a movie promotion. A photo of me in the Flower Dome from my childhood was used as a profile picture in many places—my honor book, selected PPTs. I took another photo in the same spot, but now it’s full of roses instead of the previous yellow-orange flowers. I used to be buried among the flowers, but now the flowers are at my feet. When I went to Sentosa, the sunset was setting; to watch the sunset, I didn’t go to the southernmost point of Asia that I visited as a child—I wonder if those boardwalks are still there now. I walked west, stepping on the soft sand, watching the junction of purple-red sky and water and the cargo ships docked at the shore.

I guess I’ve drawn a serious, solemn full stop. It’s completely dark now, and the elegant lights of uTown have turned on. I don’t know if I’ll have the chance to come to NUS again in the future, but I’m sure I’ll come to Singapore often in the future?
