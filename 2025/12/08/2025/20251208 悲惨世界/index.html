《悲惨世界》 Repo

从初中历史课第一次听到10周年版的《悲惨世界》序曲开始，就一直喜欢这部音乐剧作品。高中毕业后，有幸曾在23年上海文化广场看过法语版《悲惨世界》，后来又在24年上影节的院线看了电影版，25年的上影节则在院线看了10周年版的录像，今天终于有幸在上海大剧院观看40周年版的《悲惨世界》音乐会。

我自己的喜好，对于视频部分，必然是电影版最佳——毕竟有很多大制作特效，但是电影版的唱功肉耳可听不如音乐会版，真的很希望未来某一天AI能在把10周年的音频无违和地嫁接到电影版上。对于声音，我还是最喜欢10周年版本的——25周年整体的速度过快，少了庄重；法语版少了几首歌，剧情显得有点不那么连贯了。因此我最熟悉的也是10周年版的，下面的对比绝大多数也都是和10周年版本对比。

和之前一样，我也希望在观看演出前，先重听一遍10周年的专辑——但是很不巧，我这周只带了AirPods Pro回家，没法使用大馒头4听——本来多层次的音乐都被压扁了，低音的信息也丧失了，非常难受。24年上影节电影节版之前我重看了一遍原著，但是这次来不及重看一遍原著了——看了这么多遍，我想我应该也还记得大多数细节。

我这次斥巨资购置了座位非常好的票，坐在5V排正中。实际上第1，2排正中没有排座位，那里有一个延伸至台下的小T台，所以观看的时候可以离演员非常近，甚至有时恍惚间可以同时听到演员自己发出和经过麦克风收声发出的声音。序幕中冉阿让撕碎黄身份证的时候，站在T台往前扔，甚至前面两排的观众还可以捡到。有很多情绪化的表演，包括冉阿让重生、冉阿让归罪、沙威自杀、德纳第夫妇亮相，演员都会走到T台处与前排观众有眼神互动，真的能让人更加沉浸于音乐剧本身，更共情于各个角色。

我本来一直担心40周年版的演出会继承25周年版的速度过快的问题，但最终的节奏蛮符合我自己的胃口的。印象里23年的法大悲是提前录制的伴奏，这次则是现场演出，音质油好了不少。40周年的演出增添了不少的桥段（相对于10周年）。感觉开头冉阿让独白的编曲改了，变得更有层次，也与冉阿让的独白更加对应了，当然也可能是现场版音质更好。在伽弗洛什出场的时候，还增加了伽弗洛什对爱潘妮的介绍，随后沙威也重新登场介绍自己在巴黎的角色。之后还增加了德纳第夫妇在路上认出冉阿让，引来沙威，沙威驱散众人。最后婚礼前，增加了冉阿让向马吕斯介绍自己背景、德纳第夫妇阴差阳错告诉马吕斯自己是被冉阿让所救的桥段。整体上还感觉德纳第夫妇的片段变长、变多了，时不时跳出来逗逗观众，贱贱地活跃活跃气氛。演出中一些性相关的桥段的呈现比之前看的更加露骨，真的很让人担心会被删减掉，好在没有。

我对于演员和演员的唱功实际上没那么敏感，我听上去总的来说还是非常不错的，最重要的冉阿让和沙威都没有让我失望。有点可惜沙威的长发并非像10周年一样是在台上自己散开来的，而是在放冉阿让走之前台下就散开来了。我还是很想看沙威“香消玉损”自己散开长发的场面的。几位热血青年在第一幕并没有穿上他们最标志性的服装，我一开始还以为这次的服装换掉了——尤其是安灼拉标志性的红金服装，但是在第二幕就换上最熟悉的那几身衣服。这次的芳汀是一位黑人演员——其实一开始有点出戏，因为原著中的芳汀是金发美女。我觉得芳汀比较慢热，没有马上进入状态，前几句的换气非常明显，有点影响体验，但是还好最终调整过来了。芳汀是黑人，那珂赛特的演员岂不是也得是黑人——还真是。比较脸盲，开始还以为是一人分饰两角，直至最后一幕两人同时上场才意识到是两个人。这次的爱潘妮和小柯赛特竟然是华人？小柯赛特超级可爱！这一版的德纳第夫妇比较出彩，更“油”、更要面子爱显摆、更“贱”：到T台问观众讨掌声、让沙威在捉到冉阿让的时候别忘了是自己举报的、最后婚礼中用中文对观众说的“你好”、“谢谢”，忍俊不禁。

大悲的舞美也真的豪华——提几个印象比较深的点：舞台上面真的吊了一个“街垒”，在第一幕中街垒是升上去的，下面是灯，第二幕中降下来了。使用闪烁的白色灯光代表子弹，也用这种形式细细演绎了ABC之友社之死。在爱潘妮送信和沙威自杀时降下来的幽幽的路灯——真的让人感到是在阴暗的巴黎街头。以及最后的那两排蓝白红的条状灯——真的让人激情澎湃。还有最后Finale中，能明显感觉到死去的人的声音有一个“更远”的幽灵音效。但我觉得也有两点不如法大悲：法大悲在卞福汝主角出场时，用白光在舞台上方投影出了一个巨大的白色十字架，强调了他真的就是一个善良的“上帝”的化身。法大悲中多次拿出来法国国旗，尤其是在Finale中ABC之友挥舞几面法国国旗，真的让人激情澎湃——不过让一群英国人在中国挥舞法国国旗可能也有点奇怪吧。

其实本来很期待有一个大Encore的——10周年的多国冉阿让、多语言《Do you hear the people sing》和25周年的历代演员都非常不错，但想必也是首演场的专属。谁想到这一次竟然一个Encore都没有，23年的法大悲都有encore，虽然不是《Do you hear the people sing》。略失望，但无妨。最好的大悲永远是现场的大悲——期待50周年巡演，我想我应当是还会再买票的。

Les Misérables Review

I first heard the 10th-anniversary version of the Les Misérables overture during junior high school history class, and I’ve loved this musical ever since. After graduating from high school, I was fortunate enough to watch the French version of Les Misérables at Shanghai Culture Square in 2023; later, I saw the film version in theaters during the Shanghai International Film Festival in 2024, and the 10th-anniversary recording at the festival in 2025. Today, I finally got the chance to watch the 40th-anniversary concert version of Les Misérables at the Shanghai Grand Theatre.

In terms of video content, the film version is undoubtedly the best for me—after all, it features a lot of high-budget special effects. However, the vocal performance in the film version is inferior to that of the concert version to the naked ear. I truly hope that one day AI can seamlessly integrate the audio from the 10th-anniversary version into the film version without any dissonance. As for the sound quality, I still prefer the 10th-anniversary version the most: the 25th-anniversary version has an overly fast tempo and lacks solemnity; the French version omits several songs, making the plot a bit less coherent. Therefore, the 10th-anniversary version is also the one I know best, and most of the comparisons below are made against it.

As usual, I wanted to re-listen to the 10th-anniversary album before watching the show. Unfortunately, I only brought my AirPods Pro home this week and couldn’t use my Big Muff 4 to play the music—the originally layered melodies were flattened, and the bass details were lost, which was really frustrating. I reread the original novel before watching the film version at the 2024 Shanghai International Film Festival, but I didn’t have time to do so again this time. Having read it so many times, I think I still remember most of the details.

This time, I splurged on tickets with an excellent seat: row 5V, right in the middle. In fact, there are no seats in the center of rows 1 and 2; instead, there is a small T-stage extending down to the audience area. This allows viewers to be very close to the actors, and sometimes you can even hear both the actors’ natural voices and the sounds picked up by the microphones at the same time. When Jean Valjean tears up his yellow ID card during the prologue, he stands on the T-stage and throws it forward; audiences in the two rows in front can even pick it up. There are many emotionally charged performances, including Jean Valjean’s rebirth, Jean Valjean taking the blame, Javert’s suicide, and the Thénardiers’ entrance. The actors walk to the T-stage to make eye contact with the front-row audience, which really immerses viewers in the musical and helps them empathize with the characters.

I had been worried that the 40th-anniversary version would inherit the overly fast tempo of the 25th-anniversary version, but the final pacing suited my preferences quite well. I recall that the French version in 2023 used pre-recorded accompaniment, while this time it was a live performance, and the sound quality was much better. The 40th-anniversary version added quite a few new segments compared to the 10th-anniversary version. It seems the arrangement of Jean Valjean’s monologue at the beginning was changed to be more layered and better match his words; of course, it could also be due to the better sound quality of the live version. When Gavroche appears, there’s an additional scene where he introduces Éponine, and later Javert also makes a reappearance to introduce his role in Paris. Then, the Thénardiers recognize Jean Valjean on the road, which draws Javert’s attention, and Javert disperses the crowd. Finally, before the wedding, there’s a scene where Jean Valjean introduces his background to Marius, and the Thénardiers accidentally tell Marius that they were saved by Jean Valjean. Overall, the Thénardier-related scenes feel longer and more frequent; they pop up from time to time to tease the audience and liven up the atmosphere in a cheeky way. Some sexually suggestive scenes in this version are more explicit than what I’ve seen before, which makes me really worried they might be cut, but thankfully they weren’t.

I’m not particularly sensitive to the actors or their vocal performances. Overall, I thought they were very good; the most important roles, Jean Valjean and Javert, did not disappoint me. It’s a shame that Javert’s long hair wasn’t scattered naturally on stage as in the 10th-anniversary version—it was already scattered before Jean Valjean left. I really wanted to see the scene where Javert’s hair scatters as he “falls apart”. The group of revolutionaries didn’t wear their signature costumes in Act 1; I initially thought the costumes had been changed—especially Enjolras’ iconic red and gold outfit—but they put on those familiar clothes in Act 2. Fantine this time is played by a Black actress; I was a little distracted at first because Fantine in the original novel is a blonde beauty. I felt Fantine was slow to warm up and had very obvious breathing pauses in her first few lines, which affected the experience a bit, but she adjusted well eventually. If Fantine is Black, then Cosette’s actress must also be Black—and indeed she is. I’m bad at recognizing faces, so I initially thought they were the same person playing two roles; I only realized they were two different people when they appeared together in the final act. Éponine and young Cosette this time are actually of Chinese descent? Young Cosette is super cute! The Thénardier couple in this version is particularly outstanding: they’re more “slick”, more obsessed with showing off, and more “cheeky”: they ask the audience for applause from the T-stage, remind Javert not to forget that they were the ones who tipped him off about Jean Valjean, and say “hello” and “thank you” in Chinese to the audience during the wedding—it’s hilarious.

The stage design of Les Misérables is truly luxurious—here are a few highlights that left a deep impression on me: there’s actually a “barricade” suspended above the stage. In Act 1, the barricade is raised, with lights underneath; in Act 2, it is lowered. Flashing white lights are used to represent bullets, and this form is also used to vividly depict the death of the Friends of ABC. The dim streetlights that descend during Éponine’s delivery of the letter and Javert’s suicide really make you feel like you’re on the dark streets of Paris. And those two rows of blue-white-red striped lights at the end are truly exhilarating. During the finale, you can clearly hear a “farther” ghostly effect in the voices of the deceased. However, I think there are two aspects where this version falls short compared to the French version: in the French version, when Bishop Myriel appears, a giant white cross is projected above the stage using white light, emphasizing that he is truly a kind incarnation of “God”. The French version also uses the French flag multiple times, especially during the finale when the Friends of ABC wave several French flags, which is really inspiring—though it might be a bit strange to see a group of Britons waving a French flag in China.

I was actually looking forward to a grand encore: the multi-language Jean Valjeans singing Do You Hear the People Sing from the 10th-anniversary version and the cast of previous versions from the 25th-anniversary version were all great, but presumably those are exclusive to premiere shows. Who would have thought there wouldn’t be a single encore this time? Even the French version in 2023 had an encore, even if it wasn’t Do You Hear the People Sing. I’m a little disappointed, but it doesn’t matter. The best Les Misérables is always the live version—I look forward to the 50th-anniversary tour and I’m sure I’ll buy tickets again.
