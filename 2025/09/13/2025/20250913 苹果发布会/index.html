不燃的代价

过年了！过年了！

2025.9，加州来电如约而至。我认为这一次基础款有大升级，pro款有点争议，对中国大陆的影响比较大。总的来说，和之前rumor中提到的差不多。

Design is not just what it looks like and feels like. Design is how it works.
—— Steve Jobs

今年开头的视频和几年前的《了不起的设计》有点像，喜欢。我认为设计中最重要的是设计语言的一致性，我本人也非常重视这一点。但是Apple真的是把一致性做到了登峰造极——连AirPods的俯视图都能和灵动岛拼起来，令人佩服。

AirPods Pro 3

AirPods Pro是现在Apple依旧在市场上保持压倒性优势的一个品类，也是我比较喜欢的一个品类，我认为升级还算稳健，只是前代机型太强，不知能否激起大家的换机欲望。

降噪

比AirPods Pro 2强2倍！

当初 AirPods Pro 2 的时候，我还在为 AirPods Pro 1 的降噪效果震撼，无法想象再好两倍会到什么程度。

现在我同样对于再好两倍会到什么程度。

“世界上最好的降噪耳机”，没有限定词！

健康功能

可以检测效率了，rumor说是因为在 AirPods Pro 上增加了一个特殊的摄像头。

我认为是有道理的，确实很多人会在跑步的时候戴耳机，如果能够顺便记录运动数据，确实是不错的功能。

但是这样一来，它的定位就和Apple watch撞车了。不过回归原教旨主义了——iPhone会杀死iPod，但是苹果依然发售了iPhone

环大陆功能

去年喜提环大陆功能：助听器功能。因为在中国健康相关的证明比较困难。我不熟悉证件方面的政策，不好评价。但平心而论，我认为 AirPods Pro 会是一个性价比很高的助听器——性能、续航、智能化。事实上，我自己就常常把AirPods当成智能义耳。开关降噪就是控制开闭，它还能播报一些信息，像是听力版的 HUD （抬头显示）。

今年又喜提环大陆功能：实时翻译。我认为非常强且实用的，干掉了很多专门的实时翻译硬件（没有考证，但是印象里价格上都算便宜）。我本来以为它算是 Apple “翻译 app ”的子功能，但实际上似乎属于 Apple Intelligence 的子功能，因此国区没有本功能。期待该功能在各家耳机上实现，让世界的交流越来越顺畅。

其它

这次的外观小改，史诗级！但是大多数人看不出外观的改动吧？

续航由6小时改为8小时。可是我认为这其实用处不大，因为我认为对于大多数人来说，被迫摘下 AirPods Pro 的原因不是因为电量不够了，而是耳朵开始难受了。

Apple Watch

感觉 Apple Watch 系列没什么大的升级，因为看完之后回想，我竟然想不出几个新功能。

小结：

  • 对于所有机型：支持 5G ，续航增加
  • 对于 SE ：全天候显示

我认为原本对于Apple Watch SE 而言，其最大的问题就是没有全天候显示而失去了作为一款普通手表的便捷。但当现在补上了这块短板，我认为它将比数字版又更大的受众—— Apple Watch 本身也没什么用，所以性能差一些、黑边厚一些、外设差一些，问题不是很大。但是另一个问题是：Apple Watch 在我看来本身只是一个非常便宜的奢侈品，所以这样一来数字版的定位很尴尬——既没有SE的性价比，又没有 Ultra 的价格与外观。

还有件有趣的事情：Apple 官方的参数上，Apple Watch 终于不是祖传的反向虚标：数字版18h，Ultra 36h，但实际上续航每年一直有提升。这次终于标成了24h和42h。

iPhone

对全系

几年前推出的备受好评的超瓷晶面板升级了第二代。高中的时候看Leo的 iPhone 13 因为用了超瓷晶面板，所以不贴膜，手机屏幕的磨损情况也还行。但是当我和他都升级至 iPhone 15 Pro 之后，我们觉得防磨损刮削能力反而变差了，有点奇怪。希望超瓷晶面板二代能够干掉贴膜。

前置摄像头传感器变成方形的了，是好事，但也就这样。

iPhone 17

ProMotion！ProMotion！ProMotion！

高刷终于下放到了 iPhone 标准版。

记得最开始的rumor说是 iPhone 16 会上高刷，后来变成了17会上，后来又变成17不会上，因为是“Pro” Motion，然后还有人说120Hz变成90Hz，砍掉 AOD （熄屏显示）。但是最终终于是和Pro同款的ProMotion：1-120Hz，支持AOD。

但是这件事情不该夸。

忘记在哪个地方听到的观点，对于苹果来说，60Hz屏幕生产线未必比120Hz屏幕生产线便宜，因为现在市面上很少有手机还在继续使用60Hz的屏幕。但是苹果依旧使用60Hz的屏幕，只是为了确保标准版与Pro版有区分度而已——实际上是苹果的傲慢。包括iPad标准版的60Hz非全贴合屏幕也是一个道理。这让我想到了几年前的一种说法——安卓机长板非常长，短板也非常短；苹果机更加水桶——现在60Hz的屏幕在这几年竟然成了水桶上的一块非常短的短板。

目前而言，认为 iPhone 17 将会是钉子户，但也等等评测吧。

iPhone Air

认为这是一个不会大卖，有其明显缺陷的机型，但它对于苹果和中国大陆都有较大意义。

苹果的试水

薄

变得那么薄自然而然就想到这实际上是半个iPhone Fold。我认为苹果确实是在为折叠屏做准备，但是我保持我一贯的态度：折叠屏对于大多数人没有用，因为比例问题以及缺少一支笔——所以尽管不欣赏华为，但是不得不说认为华为Mate XTs是有道理的——三折叠让展平的比例变正常了，也配上了手写笔。

自研基带

iPhone 16e上使用了自研的C1基带，维持性能的同时提升了能耗[^1] 。时隔半年，苹果端出来了自研基带C1X和Wi-Fi蓝牙芯片N1，却只在Air机型上使用。我认为这带有一定的实验性质，可能是为了未来自研基带甚至是将基带集成进SoC（集成CPU）铺路，一如Apple Watch Ultra上橙色的钛合金按钮为 iPhone 15 Pro 的钛合金中框铺路。

对大陆的影响——eSIM

还是西药好使。

2015年，三星手表搭载 eSIM；

2017年 Apple Watch 跟进；

2018年，iPhone 和 iPad 跟进；

2022年，美版iPhone只搭载eSIM；

2023年，中国三大运营商暂停eSIM。

平心而论，中国的eSIM是在推进的，但是速度落后于大多数发达国家，2023年甚至开了倒车，在安卓手表端全面暂停。eSIM在中国的进度，一直听到的说法是为了反诈——但是实际上eSIM本身的安全性比实体SIM卡更高，因为更换SIM卡更容易留下痕迹们，同时也可以保留实名认证。说三大运营商在试水，研究eSIM在中国国情下如何落实，如何避免法律的空子，我是认的，但是依旧认为速度太慢——同样的水，为什么发达国家领先了这么多——纵然有国情的不同，同样在最重要的终端——手机上搭载eSIM，美国的2018和中国的2025，可相差了7年。

结果还是苹果这条鲶鱼，逼着运营商推进eSIM。

The Cost of Non-Flammability

Happy Chinese New Year! Happy Chinese New Year!

In September 2025, Apple’s event in California arrived as scheduled. I think the base model got a major upgrade this time, while the Pro models are a bit controversial; they will have a relatively big impact on mainland China. Overall, it aligns with what was mentioned in previous rumors.

Design is not just what it looks like and feels like. Design is how it works.
— Steve Jobs

This year’s keynote video is a bit similar to Great Design from a few years ago, which I like. I believe the most important aspect of design is the consistency of design language, a point I personally value highly. But Apple has truly taken consistency to an extreme — even the top-down view of AirPods can match the Dynamic Island, which is truly impressive.

AirPods Pro 3

AirPods Pro remains a category where Apple maintains a dominant market position, and it’s also a category I like. I think the upgrades are fairly solid, but since the previous generation was so good, it’s unclear whether it will spark people’s desire to upgrade.

Noise Cancellation

It’s 2 times better than AirPods Pro 2!

When AirPods Pro 2 came out, I was already amazed by the noise cancellation performance of AirPods Pro 1; I couldn’t imagine how much better it would be if it doubled again.

Now I’m equally curious about just how much better it will be.

“The world’s best noise-cancelling earbuds”, no qualifiers needed!

Health Features

They can now detect efficiency. Rumors say this is because a special camera was added to AirPods Pro.

I think this makes sense; many people wear earbuds while running, so being able to record workout data alongside would indeed be a useful feature.

But this means its positioning overlaps with that of Apple Watch. However, this aligns with Apple’s original philosophy — just as the iPhone replaced the iPod, even though Apple still sells the iPod.

Mainland China-Specific Features

Last year, a mainland China-specific feature was added: hearing aid functionality. Since obtaining health-related certifications in China is quite difficult, I’m not familiar with the relevant policies and can’t comment further. But to be fair, I think AirPods Pro would make for a very cost-effective hearing aid — in terms of performance, battery life, and smart features. In fact, I often use my AirPods as smart prosthetic ears. Switching noise cancellation on or off controls their operation, and they can also read out information, acting like a hearing version of a HUD (head-up display).

This year, another mainland China-specific feature was added: real-time translation. I find this extremely powerful and practical; it renders many dedicated real-time translation devices obsolete (I haven’t verified this, but I recall their prices are relatively low). I originally thought this was a sub-function of Apple’s “Translate app”, but it actually belongs to Apple Intelligence, which is why this feature isn’t available in the Chinese region. I look forward to seeing this feature implemented on various earbuds to make global communication smoother.

Other

The minor exterior redesign this time is epic! But most people probably won’t notice the changes, right?

Battery life was increased from 6 hours to 8 hours. However, I don’t think this is very useful, because for most people, the reason they take their AirPods Pro off isn’t due to low battery, but because their ears start to feel uncomfortable.

Apple Watch

It feels like the Apple Watch series didn’t get any major upgrades; after watching the keynote, I couldn’t recall many new features at all.

Summary:

  • For all models: support for 5G, increased battery life
  • For SE models: always-on display

I think the biggest issue with the Apple Watch SE was the lack of an always-on display, which reduced its convenience as a regular watch. Now that this shortcoming has been addressed, I think it will appeal to an even wider audience than the standard model — since the Apple Watch itself isn’t very useful anyway, so having slightly worse performance, thicker bezels, and inferior peripherals isn’t a big deal. But there’s another problem: in my view, the Apple Watch is essentially a very cheap luxury item, so the positioning of the standard model has become awkward — it has neither the cost-effectiveness of the SE nor the price and appearance of the Ultra.

There’s also an interesting thing: on Apple’s official specs, the Apple Watch finally stopped using its old habit of underreporting battery life: the standard model now has 18 hours, the Ultra has 36 hours, when in reality battery life has been improving every year. This time, the numbers were finally updated to 24 hours and 42 hours.

iPhone

For All Models

The Ceramic Shield panel, which received high praise when introduced a few years ago, has been upgraded to the second generation. Back in high school, I saw Leo’s iPhone 13 use Ceramic Shield and not require a screen protector, and the screen wear was minimal. But after both of us upgraded to the iPhone 15 Pro, we found the scratch resistance had actually worsened, which is a bit strange. Hopefully, the second-generation Ceramic Shield will eliminate the need for a screen protector.

The front camera sensor has become square-shaped; this is a good change, but that’s about it.

iPhone 17

ProMotion! ProMotion! ProMotion!

High refresh rate has finally made its way to the standard iPhone model.

I remember the initial rumors said the iPhone 16 would get a high refresh rate, then it shifted to the 17, then it was said the 17 wouldn’t have it because it was “Pro” Motion, and some people said the 120Hz refresh rate would be cut to 90Hz and AOD (always-on display) would be removed. But in the end, it’s the same ProMotion as the Pro models: 1-120Hz, with support for AOD.

But we shouldn’t praise this.

I heard a viewpoint somewhere that for Apple, a 60Hz screen production line might not be cheaper than a 120Hz one, since very few phones on the market still use 60Hz screens. But Apple still used 60Hz screens just to maintain a distinction between the standard and Pro models — this is actually Apple’s arrogance. The same applies to the 60Hz non-full laminated screen on the standard iPad. This reminds me of a saying from a few years ago — Android phones have very long strong points but very short weak points; Apple products are more well-rounded — but now a 60Hz screen has become a very short weak point among Apple products over the past few years.

For now, I think the iPhone 17 will keep users from upgrading for a long time, but we’ll have to wait for reviews to be sure.

iPhone Air

I think this is a model that won’t sell in large numbers and has obvious flaws, but it’s quite significant for both Apple and mainland China.

Apple’s Experiment

Thin Design

Being so thin naturally makes one think this is half of an iPhone Fold. I think Apple is indeed preparing for foldable screens, but I stick to my usual stance: foldable screens are useless for most people due to aspect ratio issues and the lack of a stylus — so even though I don’t admire Huawei, I have to admit Huawei’s Mate XT makes sense: the triple-fold design makes the flat aspect ratio normal, and it comes with a stylus.

Self-developed Modem

The iPhone 16e uses Apple’s self-developed C1 modem, which improves energy efficiency while maintaining performance[^1]. Half a year later, Apple released the self-developed C1X modem and N1 Wi-Fi/Bluetooth chip, but only for the Air model. I think this has a certain experimental nature; it might be paving the way for future self-developed modems or even integrating the modem into the SoC (integrated CPU), just as the orange titanium button on the Apple Watch Ultra paved the way for the titanium midframe on the iPhone 15 Pro.

Impact on Mainland China — eSIM

Western medicine works better.

In 2015, Samsung watches adopted eSIM;

In 2017, Apple Watch followed suit;

In 2018, iPhone and iPad followed suit;

In 2022, US versions of iPhone only supported eSIM;

In 2023, China’s three major carriers suspended eSIM support.

To be fair, eSIM adoption in China is progressing, but the speed lags behind most developed countries. In 2023, there was even a step backward, with eSIM support suspended across all Android watches. The commonly heard explanation for eSIM’s slow progress in China is anti-fraud measures — but in reality, eSIM is more secure than physical SIM cards, as replacing a SIM card leaves more traces, and eSIM can still retain real-name authentication. I accept the claim that the three major carriers are testing and researching how to implement eSIM in China’s context and avoid legal loopholes, but I still think the pace is too slow — why are developed countries so far ahead when it comes to this? Even accounting for national differences, having eSIM on the most important device — the phone — took the US 2018 and China 2025, a 7-year gap.

In the end, it’s Apple, the disruptive force, that pressured carriers to promote eSIM.

Today, a white paper from China Unicom circulated online, sparking many rumors. I found the relevant part related to eSIM as follows:

There are many interpretations online, but I think this document is actually very vague. What does “bidirectional SIM-card locking, cross-border card writing disabled” mean? After the iPhone Air was released, Apple’s official website provided clearer information[^2]. To summarize:

  1. Foreign versions of iPhone cannot use eSIM from domestic carriers
    • What if foreigners visiting China can’t use eSIM? Travel data plans?
    • If an international student buys an eSIM phone abroad, how can they use it when they return to China?
    • Will the market for foreign versions of pure eSIM phones in China shrink? I look forward to seeing what the merchants in Huaqiangbei can do.
    • Don’t say we still have physical SIMs, so we don’t need to worry about these issues — it’s very likely that eSIM will be fully adopted within a few years.
  2. Overseas eSIM can be activated, provided the user is outside China
    • I think this is reasonable and acceptable.
    • Note that it’s “activation”; this means you can buy a foreign eSIM in China, write the information to the phone, and activate it once you arrive abroad. That’s logical!
  3. eSIM must be processed at offline business halls
    • I think this is redundant. Online processing is one of the biggest advantages of eSIM. Online real-name verification is possible and poses no security risks.
    • In fact, the previous conspiracy theory that eSIM wasn’t adopted in China was to protect the interests of carriers, since online processes are too transparent.

Other

The back panel material has also been upgraded from ceramic to Ceramic Shield, which significantly improves scratch resistance on the back. But I still don’t feel confident using it without a case regarding drop protection.

I’m really worried about the battery life. A larger 6.5-inch screen requires a bigger battery, and Apple even designed a magnetic charger specifically for the Air — it all feels like patching up a problem.

I thought the Air would be the 17 Air, replacing the Plus and Mini models, so it should use the same chip as the standard model. But seeing the A19 Pro chip and starting price of 7999, I realized it’s actually positioned as a Pro model.

iPhone 17 Pro / Pro Max

This is also a hard-to-judge model; its design philosophy is really similar to Android — starting with the “Pro” font on the promotional poster.

Photography

First of all, what I’m most excited about is the 13-200mm zoom range. Apple upgraded the 12-megapixel 5x telephoto lens to a 48-megapixel 4x telephoto lens, which can be cropped to a 12-megapixel 8x lens. A 13-200mm zoom range is really great!

My camera is a RX100 VII, with a 24-200mm range. The reason I firmly chose a compact camera over a mirrorless one back then was that I probably wouldn’t take a mirrorless camera out with me. The main reason I chose a compact camera over a phone was optical zoom. But now I realize:

  1. Both have very limited aperture adjustment capabilities;
  2. The RX100 VII might offer more color grading flexibility, but I don’t grade photos often;
  3. iPhone’s HDR is more convenient and produces better results;
  4. iPhone has better and more convenient night photography.

This makes me truly agree that compact cameras should be phased out.

(As for why I didn’t have this feeling when Android phones had large zoom ranges, it’s mainly because I’m an Apple user — I only feel the impact when it affects me directly.)

(But compact cameras are really elegant.)

(I look forward to the Sony A7C3, but large-format zoom lenses for full-frame cameras are too bulky; I’m torn.)

(Maybe I should get a large-aperture prime lens, but I’m not professional enough for that; it’s just for hobby purposes.)

But!

But to achieve better imaging performance, the deco (the raised part housing the cameras) has taken this shape, paying homage to Xiaomi’s 11 Ultra!

Ugly, so ugly… I didn’t expect the rumors to be true…

Heat Dissipation

This was the highlight of the keynote: the addition of a vapor chamber, which is great!

Switching back to an aluminum unibody for better heat dissipation is acceptable to me; I have no preference for stainless steel or titanium.

But the join between the unibody and the second-generation Ceramic Shield back panel is really ugly — this is a compromise for heat dissipation and MagSafe.

I believe some people support MagSafe and hope to return to a “midframe” design instead of a unibody. Others support the vapor chamber and think few people use MagSafe, so they want the Ceramic Shield panel removed and replaced entirely with aluminum.

Which side are you on?

Other

No iPad or Mac was released this time, so I expect there to be a launch event in October.

Not all of this keynote was recorded at Apple Park; parts were filmed at various Apple Stores (I guess in California and New York? I couldn’t tell). I look forward to future keynotes being recorded at Apple Stores around the world.

Craig didn’t appear in the TT segment; I look forward to him introducing the iPad at the October event.
