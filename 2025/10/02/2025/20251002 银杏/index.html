银杏的凋零

今天是2025年10月1日，今晚我与母亲住到了jt，想住几天。其实原本我是想一个人住两天的，不过恰逢外公外婆去崇明旅游，我和母亲便一起住了过来。来之前，去了一趟旁边新开的盒马超市，拎着大包小包，分了两次才将所有的东西都搬上楼。搬完之后，我下意识地向阳台走去——实际上，不来璟庭，我根本不会记起来这一棵银杏树的，但是每次来了，我总想去看一下它，确认它还在。

2014年，我在曲阳路学而思学习奥数。学而思的对面，我们停车的地方，有一排银杏树。银杏树的果子掉下来，落在地上，生出了根，却没扎进土里——想来也不过是垃圾桶或是除草机的命运。我与父亲把小苗带了回来，栽进土里——一晃已经十一年了。照理说，十一年的树，也应当不小了，不过想必是因为种在盆里憋屈，它也只有人的手指那么粗。曾经还一直开玩笑“等买了大‘别野’，有了大院子，就把它栽在土里”，没想到“别野”影子也没有，树却先枯死了。

近几年，到了冬天，它的叶子是照常落光，但春天则越长越少——想必是衰败的前兆吧。搬离璟庭之后，这里无人打理，只有每几周母亲来浇点水。我记得我曾经说过想给它换个盆，松松土，但也在细碎的生活之中，遗忘搁置了此事。去年冬天，银杏落下了最后的叶子，今年春天却没有长出叶子，而我竟然直到今年初秋才知道。

银杏死了，光秃秃地立着，旁边散落几片去年冬天落下的树叶。阳台上的仙人掌无人照料，却也郁郁葱葱，像是一片沙漠。沙漠曾经也是绿洲，也是海洋，但现在只有仙人掌和沙子。

该走的走了，该去的去了，该变的变了。人最怕变化，求平淡安稳，但实际上却又被命运洪流推去陌生的未来。熟悉的当下，总是渐渐变得陌生——陌生本身不让人难过，甚至让人兴奋，但熟悉与陌生的对比，总让人生出物是人非之感。我最怕失去，甚至为此不愿意拥有，这样的我面对这些变化，也只能用过记忆来对抗世事的消亡，用文字来对抗记忆的消亡。

苏子曰：“盖将自其变者而观之，则天地曾不能以一瞬；自其不变者而观之，则物与我皆无尽也。”可我觉得“变”的悲哀不在于“恒定”之“短暂”，而在于“我”仍为“我”。回望之后，还是需要转过头，往前走。

再回首，恍然如梦。

The Withering of Ginkgo Trees

Today is October 1, 2025. I’m staying at JT with my mother for a few days; originally I had planned to stay alone for two days, but since my grandparents were on a trip to Chongming, my mother and I decided to come together. Before coming, we stopped by the newly opened Hema supermarket nearby. Carrying heavy shopping bags, it took us two trips to haul everything upstairs. After moving in, I instinctively walked to the balcony — in fact, if I hadn’t come to Jingting, I wouldn’t have remembered this ginkgo tree at all. But every time I visit, I always want to see it and confirm that it’s still there.

In 2014, I studied Olympiad math at Xueersi on Quyang Road. Across from Xueersi, where we parked our car, there was a row of ginkgo trees. The fruits of the ginkgo trees fell to the ground, sprouted roots, but never took hold in the soil — most likely ending up in the trash or being crushed by a lawn mower. My father and I took the saplings home and planted them in the soil — eleven years have passed in the blink of an eye. Logically, an eleven-year-old tree should be quite large, but perhaps because it was confined to a pot, it’s only as thick as a human finger. We used to joke, “Once we buy a big villa with a spacious yard, we’ll plant it in the ground.” Unexpectedly, we never got the villa, but the tree withered first.

In recent years, its leaves would fall off as usual in winter, but fewer and fewer new leaves grew in spring — presumably a sign of decline. After we moved out of Jingting, no one took care of it except for my mother, who watered it every few weeks. I remember I once said I wanted to repot it and loosen the soil, but that plan was forgotten amid the busyness of daily life. Last winter, the ginkgo tree shed its last leaves, and it didn’t produce any new leaves this spring — I didn’t even realize this until early autumn this year.

The ginkgo tree is dead now, standing bare with a few leaves from last winter scattered around it. The cactus on the balcony, left unattended, is thriving lushly, looking like a desert. Deserts were once oases and oceans, but now all that remains are cacti and sand.

What needs to go has gone, what needs to come has come, and what needs to change has changed. People fear change the most and crave stability, but in reality, they are swept along by the current of fate into an unfamiliar future. The familiar present gradually becomes strange — strangeness itself isn’t sad, and may even be exciting, but the contrast between the familiar and the unfamiliar always evokes a sense of things remaining the same while people change. I fear loss the most, to the point that I’m reluctant to possess anything; faced with these changes, I can only use memories to resist the disappearance of the world and words to resist the fading of memories.

Su Shi said, “If we view things from the perspective of change, then heaven and earth cannot remain static for even a moment; if we view them from the perspective of constancy, then all things and we ourselves are endless.” But I think the sadness of “change” lies not in the “transience” of “constancy”, but in the fact that “I” remain “me”. Looking back, I still need to turn my head and move forward.

Looking back, it feels like a dream.
