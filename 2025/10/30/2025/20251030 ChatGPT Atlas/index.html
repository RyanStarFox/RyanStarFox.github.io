Chrome的新晋挑战者——ChatGPT Atlas浏览器小试牛刀

引

2025.10.22，ChatGPT Atlas横空出世。目前仅支持Mac端客户端。恰好我自己就是Mac，因此在今天小小地尝试了一下。

对我来说，我先入为主地认为我不会把主力换成Atlas，因为迁移浏览器的成本非常高。但是如果Atlas有比传统浏览器更加显著的优势，我认为我还是非常乐于更换的，但是简单使用下来，发现由于响应速度和使用习惯的原因，我还是会继续使用传统的浏览器。

ChatGPT Atlas

传统浏览器使用的是搜索引擎，按下搜索键之后会立即出结果。但是Atlas默认是AI生成总结，要等AI先思考，再一个一个地吐Token。就我自己的经验来看，现代人都是非常没有耐心的。1s生成的搜索结果页，我们可能就往下划一下，看看有没有什么需要的，还有一些阅读和等待的耐心。但是看到一串还没讲完的话，好比好视频不能两倍速，大家很可能就切屏分心做别的事情了。

在检索资料方面，搜索引擎的精确性和真实性依然强于AI。我既然都使用浏览器了，说明我还是希望对于检索拥有更高的自主权——如果仅仅是需要获得大概的信息，我直接使用AI就是了。其实对于AI和搜索引擎的平衡，我认为谷歌“AI”概括+网页展示的模式和UI比Atlas做得更好，我也更加习惯**。**

使用习惯上，Atlas和传统浏览器也有所区别，我是一个喜欢在搜索栏使用“搜索引擎名+搜索内容”中指定搜索引擎搜索的人，例如我已经习惯了在搜索栏中使用“豆瓣+电影名”来直接在豆瓣中搜索电影。但是目前为止，好像Atlas还没有这个功能，而是总是默认使用AI。

事实上，我注意到，其实对于最基础的搜索功能而言，无非是OpenAI自己做了一个“搜索引擎”，但它是不开放的，把搜索结果页的链接拷贝到别的浏览器，是无法被识别的。这一点上感到OpenAI做得不够“open”，这个阻碍虽然确保了用户在自己手里，但是也让别的用户投奔Atlas变得困难。

Atlas的搜索结果现在和谷歌很像，我不清楚Atlas有没有使用AI功能筛选出了那些更有用、更相关的网页——我认为这个筛选才是AI的优势所在，省去了人工筛选的经历。不仅如此，这样以来AI搜索结果的倾向性也比搜索引擎更难操控，或许还降低了bias？谷歌的搜索算法会有一些攻击混淆方式，对于ChatGPT的搜索结果，会不会也有一些攻击方式呢？期待一下发展。

但必须承认，浏览器不止搜索引擎，它能够识别网页具体内容并帮助用户作出响应，这一点可以极高地提高工作效率。网页内容的通用性，又让所有的网页App可以因此获得很强的功能。已经可以想见到AI写文档、AI写email、AI画思维导图、AI写作业等等潜在的功能了。这一点可喜可贺。但是我的疑问是，这不相当于就是一个浏览器插件嘛？还是说Atlas中AI对浏览器的掌控更加底层，因此能做更多的事情？

关于Atlas能不能撼动Chrome，我认为还很悬，因为目前它的表现在我看来不尽如人意，况且目前Atlas仍旧在使用Chromium核心。

我构想的AI浏览器

就我自己构想，我认为我心中理想的AI浏览器是这样的：浏览器本身能够随时引用搜索结果、浏览历史作为上下文，就好比Cursor一样，除此之外，各项功能和传统浏览器没有区别。（这么一来Edge+Copilot其实有点接近？）浏览器可以自定义搜索引擎。

因此也要构想一个理想的AI搜索引擎：首先，对于搜索的文本，AI应当自动判断我的意图，决定回答AI对话还是网页搜索结果，两者也可以手动切换。对于后者，我还是认为谷歌目前AI梗概+网页是比较理想的response。但同时搜索引擎的搜索结果让AI根据默认或者自定义的Prompt，筛选真正语意相关的网页。

期待一下AI浏览器的发展。

Chrome’s new challenger — A brief try of the ChatGPT Atlas browser

Introduction

On October 22, 2025, ChatGPT Atlas was launched. Currently, it only supports Mac clients. Coincidentally, I use a Mac, so I gave it a quick try today.

I initially assumed I wouldn’t switch to Atlas as my primary browser, since the cost of migrating browsers is very high. However, if Atlas offers significantly more advantages than traditional browsers, I would be happy to make the switch. After using it briefly, though, I found that due to its response speed and my existing usage habits, I will continue using traditional browsers.

ChatGPT Atlas

Traditional browsers use search engines, which display results immediately after you press the search key. But Atlas defaults to AI-generated summaries: it waits for the AI to think before outputting tokens one by one. In my experience, modern people have very little patience. For a search results page generated in 1 second, we might scroll down slightly to see if what we need is there, and we still have the patience to read and wait. But when we see an unfinished stream of text—like a video that can’t be played at double speed—we are likely to switch tabs and focus on other things.

When it comes to retrieving information, search engines still outperform AI in terms of accuracy and authenticity. Since I use a browser, it means I want greater autonomy over my searches—if I just need general information, I can simply use an AI tool. In fact, regarding the balance between AI and search engines, I think Google’s model of AI summaries + web page displays, along with its UI, is better implemented than Atlas, and I am more accustomed to it.**

In terms of usage habits, Atlas also differs from traditional browsers. I am someone who likes to specify a search engine in the search bar by typing “search engine name + search query”; for example, I’m used to typing “Douban + movie name” to search for movies directly on Douban. So far, however, Atlas does not seem to support this feature and always defaults to using AI.

In fact, I noticed that for the most basic search function, OpenAI has essentially created a “search engine” of its own, but it is not open: if you copy the link to a search results page and open it in another browser, it won’t be recognized. I feel OpenAI is not “open” enough on this point. While this ensures users stay within their ecosystem, it also makes it difficult for other users to switch to Atlas.

Atlas’s search results currently resemble those of Google. I’m not sure whether Atlas uses AI to filter out more useful and relevant web pages—I think this filtering is where AI’s advantage lies, as it saves the trouble of manual screening. Moreover, this makes the bias in AI search results harder to manipulate than that of search engines; perhaps it even reduces bias? Google’s search algorithm has some ways to exploit vulnerabilities, so I wonder if there will be similar exploits for ChatGPT’s search results? I look forward to seeing how it develops.

But it must be admitted that a browser is more than just a search engine: it can recognize the specific content of web pages and help users take action, which can greatly improve work efficiency. The universality of web content also enables all web apps to gain powerful functionality. We can already envision potential features like AI document writing, AI email drafting, AI mind map creation, and AI homework help. This is certainly welcome. My question is: isn’t this just a browser extension? Or does the AI in Atlas have deeper control over the browser, allowing it to do more?

As for whether Atlas can challenge Chrome, I think it’s still uncertain, as its current performance is less than satisfactory to me, and it still uses the Chromium core.

My vision for an AI browser

In my own vision, the ideal AI browser would be able to reference search results and browsing history as context at any time, similar to Cursor. Other than that, its functions would be no different from those of a traditional browser. (In this regard, Edge + Copilot is actually quite close?) The browser would also allow users to customize search engines.

Therefore, we also need to envision an ideal AI search engine: first, for any search query, the AI should automatically determine my intent and decide whether to provide an AI response or web search results, with the ability to switch between the two manually. For the latter, I still think Google’s current model of AI summaries + web pages is an ideal response. At the same time, the search engine’s results should be filtered by the AI based on default or custom prompts to show only truly semantically relevant web pages.

I look forward to the development of AI browsers.
