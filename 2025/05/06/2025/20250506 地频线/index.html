这段文字写于地频线结束之后一天的傍晚，我坐在包玉刚图书馆3楼的沙发上。这对我来说更像是一个寻常的傍晚。于我而言，地频线是一段叛逆，因此在一切尘埃落定之后，显得竟然如此不真实。昨晚4点，已闻啼鸟，我回寝室，最后一次把阿姨叫醒开门，最后一次说“对不起，活动结束了，这是最后一次晚归了。”我一个人坐在自己的位置上，把除了文件传输助手的71个置顶逐一取消，翻找了好久自己以前的头像。想到bhy说“你敢想象嘛，第二天，菁菁堂会一点痕迹也没有。”天蒙蒙亮了，恍惚中我觉得或许此刻只是因为被抛起的失重惊醒，翻个身又该睡了。

昨晚刚刚从大连飞回来，这是上交大第28届歌手大赛——地频线结束之后的第一次旅行，与导演组一道。觉得对于这一段经历，还需要写一点什么。

去年12月，自从成为歌手大赛的大岛之后，我就已经开始期待被抛起来的一刻了——只是希望尘埃落定。那时的我，急躁、不知所措。我始终认为自己做不了一个领导者——我做不了乔布斯，我只能做沃兹尼亚克，即使如今也依旧深信如此。但因种种原因我最终被推到了这个位置。我一直都战战兢兢：我参与这个活动只是因为自己的体验主义，只是在尝试偏离自己原本生活方式，可是我不敢告诉别人我的想法，我认为它很自私，怎么能把把大家投入这么多精力的一件事情仅仅视作自己对“陌生体验感”的追求。我害怕我做不好：导演组的各位都专于自己负责的部分，我害怕自己不学无术，因此无所事事，爱莫能助，我怎能成为名义上的“大岛”？

说来奇怪，复赛结束之后，我反而比决赛结束之后更有一种释然的感觉。决赛之前的我，反而有种高三之感——同样日夜与一群人为了一个共同目标做事；同样发着疯，笑点很低，苦中作乐；同样希望尽快尘埃落定，又不愿结束。待到决赛中与结束之后，甚至在决赛现场彩排的那个周末，我更深刻的感受反而是不真实感：为了地频线，暂时地放下了很多的东西：浅层的是自己的学业与日程，更深层的是自己的生活方式、处事的习惯——尤其是我的思维，人在过度疲劳之时，是真的会不想动一点脑子、不想听一点声音、不想见一点东西、不想和一个人交流的，此时只盼一片白茫茫而又虚无的清净。活动结束之后便一头扎入真实的学业之海——直至一周的备考和随后的期中考试之后，当天又与导演组一道前去机场，奔向大连的更加真实的海。

在地频线中，获得了很多的关系——这是除了陌生的体验感之外，我最想从地频线中得到的东西。但它也同样自私，我不敢告诉别人，即使它听上去又是如此正当。但结束之后，我可以纵情享受这些关系，这些联结了：我们导演组很早就相约大连，终于在五一实现——一直叫嚷着想吃大连的海鲜，真正吃到口中，那种实在感竟然有些奇怪。通过地频线，也与许多工作人员和歌手相熟识，其中不乏一些留学生——我很享受与来自孟加拉国的92%e人Mon聊天，通过他的眼睛，我看到了不一样的，但是同样有趣的世界。

地频线是与我寻常生活的巨大的偏离，日后怕是不会再有吧？彩色的记忆变得黑白，对此的思绪也逐渐混沌，但这段故事从时间之筛中留下，已在心中扎根。

This text was written on the evening of the day after Horython concluded, as I sat on a sofa on the 3rd floor of the P.Y. Koo Library. For me, this was just an ordinary evening. To me, Horython was an act of rebellion, so it feels strangely unreal now that everything has settled. At 4 AM last night, when the birds had already started chirping, I returned to my dormitory, woke Auntie up one last time to open the door, and said for the final time, “I’m sorry, the event is over; this is my last late return.” I sat alone at my desk, unpinned all 71 chat threads except for File Transfer Assistant, and spent a long time searching for my old profile picture. I thought of what bhy said: “Can you imagine? The next day, there won’t be a single trace left of Jingjing Hall.” As dawn broke, in a daze, I felt like I might have been woken up by the sensation of weightlessness from being tossed around; I turned over and figured I should go back to sleep.

I had just flown back from Dalian last night — this was my first trip after the 28th SJTU Singing Competition, Horython, and I went with the directing team. I feel I still need to write something about this experience.

Since December last year, after becoming the “Great Island” of the singing competition, I had been looking forward to the moment when everything would settle — I was impatient and at a loss. I always thought I couldn’t be a leader: I couldn’t be a Jobs, I could only be a Wozniak, and I still firmly believe that today. But for various reasons, I was ultimately pushed into this role. I was always nervous: I participated in this event purely out of my own experientialism, just trying to deviate from my usual way of life, but I didn’t dare to tell others my thoughts — I thought it was selfish to treat something that required so much effort from everyone as merely a pursuit of “novel experiences” for myself. I was afraid I wouldn’t do well: everyone on the directing team was focused on their respective responsibilities, and I feared I would be incompetent, doing nothing to help — how could I be the nominal “Great Island”?

Strangely enough, after the semi-finals, I felt more relieved than after the finals. Before the finals, I felt like I was in my senior year of high school — working with a group of people toward a common goal day and night; going crazy together, laughing easily even in hardship; hoping to get things settled quickly while not wanting it to end. After the finals and even during the weekend rehearsals for the finals, what I felt more profoundly was a sense of unreality: for the sake of Horython, I temporarily put aside many things — superficially, my studies and schedule; more deeply, my lifestyle and ways of handling things — especially my thinking patterns. When a person is overly fatigued, they truly don’t want to use their brain, hear sounds, see things, or talk to anyone; all they want is a blank, void-like tranquility. After the event ended, I plunged straight into the sea of real academic work — until after a week of exam preparation and the subsequent midterms, I went to the airport with the directing team that same day to head to the even more real sea in Dalian.

Through Horython, I gained many relationships — this was what I wanted most from the event besides the novel experiences. But it’s also selfish; I didn’t dare to tell others, even though it sounds so justified. But now that it’s over, I can fully enjoy these relationships and connections: our directing team had planned to meet in Dalian early on, and we finally made it happen during May Day — I had been clamoring to eat Dalian seafood, and when I actually tasted it, the sense of tangibility was oddly surprising. Through Horython, I also got to know many staff members and singers, including several international students — I enjoyed chatting with Mon from Bangladesh; through his eyes, I saw a different, yet equally interesting world.

Horython was a huge deviation from my ordinary life; I doubt I’ll ever experience anything like it again. The colorful memories have turned black and white, and my thoughts about it are gradually becoming muddled, but this story has survived the sieve of time and taken root in my heart.
