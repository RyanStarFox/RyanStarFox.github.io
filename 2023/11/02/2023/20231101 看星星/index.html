看星星

很巧，很多很喜欢的作品都是星际题材：星际争霸、星球大战、星际牛仔……无数的星星各有各的神秘与朦胧，对于我而言，星星对我的吸引不仅仅在于对未知的好奇，更因其因人类认知的永远无法企及全部星星的崇高与悲壮。

我是什么时候开始看星星的呢？应当是高一学期初那几个夜晚。当时和Bill臭味相投，已经忘了是什么契机，发现上中竟然能看到很多星星，于是乎每天晚上晚自修结束一起去上中操场，躺在软软的草地上面，一颗一颗地认星星，最后爬起来的时候发现屁股和背全都湿掉了。当时就和他聊，说多少年后，这一段一起看星星的时光可能会模糊朦胧，可能会分不清楚究竟是梦还是真实，但绝对不敢把这段时光忘掉，因为这一段时光太珍贵了。从2020年10月的天空开始几个月的天空当时都十分熟悉。火星、木星、土星、牛郎、织女、天津一、天津四、毕宿五、五车二、五车三、天狼、北落师门、还有整个参宿。这些只在书上看到的名字突然在现实世界有了一个实体。这就好比我抬起头，第一次发现那崇高的未知世界的大门竟然有一道缝，缝里洒出来一束光，我进不去那个世界，却可以沐浴在光芒之中。

后来大操场翻修了，在高中期间再也没开，我和Bill也停止了看星星。很久不看星星，对于星空也陌生了。高三那年的中秋节，我和我的母亲半夜出去散步，想找一个好地方赏月，无奈到处都是灯光，钠灯的晕影无情地遮盖了月之皎洁。后来不知是谁想出对策，可以去楼顶赏月。我们到了楼顶，地平线附近依旧是橙色的，但天空暗了很多，不仅月景更加干净，星星也看到的更多了。那是木星、土星。我惊讶地发现自己依约能够识别出一些星星，使用手机软件对这天空一看，大多都是熟悉的名字。那一瞬间，感到星星是重逢故友。但人之渺小有何德何能与星星为友？能与星星连接，与自然连接，与崇高连接，心中反而怯怯的、空空的。

之前听过一个理论：怯魅的过程实际上是斩断了人与世界的连接感。就好比告诉小孩圣诞老人不存在，礼物是父母偷偷送的，小孩依旧收到礼物，但是心里总会空落落的。（应该是《刘擎》里的观点？）我发现我或许就在用与星星的连接去填补内心的空落，我在尝试与更大的世界产生联系，让自己的心与宇宙交融。

暑假里去了西藏，在高原上，我们两次出去看星空。我第一次用肉眼看到了银河，看到了更多的星星。那一瞬间的崇高感至今时时浮现，感觉那是我和世界联系得最紧密的时刻。

一起来看星星吧！

Stargazing

Coincidentally, many of my favorite works are set in space-themed universes: StarCraft, Star Wars, Cowboy Bebop… Each star holds its own mystery and obscurity. For me, the appeal of stars lies not only in curiosity about the unknown but also in their sublime and tragic nature—a grandeur that human cognition can never fully grasp.

When did I start stargazing? It was probably during those few nights at the beginning of my first year of high school. I got along well with Bill back then; I’ve forgotten what prompted us to go, but we discovered that we could see many stars from our school. So every night after evening self-study, we’d head to the school playground together, lie on the soft grass, and identify stars one by one. When we finally got up, we’d find our backsides and backs were completely wet. I told him at the time that years later, those moments of stargazing together might become hazy, and we might not be able to tell if they were dreams or reality—but we would never dare to forget them, because those moments were far too precious. The skies from October 2020 onward became very familiar to us over the next few months: Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Altair, Vega, Deneb, Aldebaran, Capella, Rigel, Sirius, Fomalhaut, and the entire Orion constellation. These names that I had only read about in books suddenly took on a physical form in the real world. It was as if I looked up and found for the first time a crack in the door to that lofty unknown world; a beam of light shone through the crack. I couldn’t enter that world, but I could bask in its glow.

Later, the main playground was renovated and closed for the rest of my high school years, and Bill and I stopped stargazing. After going a long time without watching the stars, I grew unfamiliar with the night sky. During the Mid-Autumn Festival of my senior year, my mother and I went out for a walk at midnight to find a good spot to admire the moon. Unfortunately, lights were everywhere, and the hazy glow of sodium lamps mercilessly obscured the moon’s brightness. Eventually, someone came up with an idea: we could go to the rooftop to view the moon. When we reached the rooftop, the horizon was still orange, but the sky was much darker; not only was the moonlit view clearer, but we could see more stars too—Jupiter and Saturn. To my surprise, I could still identify some stars as expected. When I used a mobile app to scan the sky, most of the stars had familiar names. In that moment, I felt as if the stars were old friends I reunited with. But how could a mere mortal like me deserve to befriend the stars? Connecting with the stars, with nature, with the sublime, left me feeling timid and empty-hearted.

I once heard a theory: the process of disenchantment actually severs the sense of connection between humans and the world. It’s like telling a child that Santa Claus doesn’t exist and that the gifts are secretly given by their parents; the child still receives the gifts, but feels a sense of emptiness inside. (I think this is a view from Liu Qing’s writings?) I realized that I might be using my connection to the stars to fill the void in my heart; I’m trying to establish a link with a larger world and let my heart merge with the universe.

During the summer vacation, I went to Tibet, where we went stargazing twice on the plateau. For the first time, I saw the Milky Way with my naked eye and observed even more stars. The sense of sublime awe I felt in that moment still lingers in my mind; it felt like the closest I’ve ever been to the world.

Let’s go stargazing together!
