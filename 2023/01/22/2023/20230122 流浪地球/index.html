是好的

流浪地球2确实比1好很多，首当其冲，感情戏不尴尬，不刻意，不在规训，非常真实，尤其是图桓宇在14年之后重新见到丫丫的那种激动的神态，刘德华真的演得太好了，很令人动容。还有最后老航天人牺牲自己用机械引线引爆核弹，也是非常让人敬畏的。而且这部电影的最大危机不像1那样来的莫名其妙（片尾也解释了1的危机的来源，也算是填坑？），剧情更加完整、连续。

科幻特效方面，依旧保持了1的水准。各种车辆、飞船都很有机械感，也有设计的连续性。尤其是片首太空电梯的部分，这或许是大银幕上首次展现太空电梯？还有方舟号空间站坠毁的镜头，真的太震撼了！唯有两个特效我不太满意；月球引爆核弹时核弹的动画缺少细节，看上去不像是核弹，只是一个个白色的光球膨胀开来而已。或许没有氧气环境下的核武爆炸是这样的？求科普。另外，月球爆炸那段，总感觉一开始给的远视核弹爆炸的画面没做好。虽然核弹爆炸的范围确实就这么小，但是它的连锁反应是巨大的，应当同一个镜头继续刻画月球爆炸的场景。可是实际上，月球爆炸的镜头被拉近了，导致月球坍塌的画面似乎有些小气。

缺点也是有的。我尤其不喜欢一个个过场中“xx事件，倒计时xx时间”。它把对于电影的沉浸感完全破坏掉了，就像是在提醒我：这不是真的，你是在电影院里！而且，它像官方剧透一样，我还在想接下来会发生什么呢，它就告诉我了，这不就没有悬疑感了吗？

这部电影让我失望的一点在于它没有把上传智能的伦理问题探讨。上传智能的伦理问题应该是很有讨论空间的，而且也确实是一个电影的主线之一。关于上传智能是否是人？上传的人和物理的人之间的关系？上传智能被复制了怎么办？似乎电影并没有深入地讨论下去。而且既然上传智能已经几乎成功了，只是缺一个合适地运行环境（550C/550W），为什么不把没能进入地下城地人变成上传智能？这些没有肉体的上传智能和地下城居民地矛盾？这其实可以挖得挺深的。

我觉得电影的色彩也没有做好。不知是否是我去的影院的问题，总感觉对比度好低，亮度也略微有些低，像没有开启HDR一样，总让人感觉不是很舒服

总之，片子是好片子，值得去巨幕厅一看，没有失掉1的水准。有提升空间，但总体还是不错的。

可惜片尾不如1的震撼。1的片尾流浪地球在书页中间飞太美了！感觉可以超过漫威的片头。

It’s a good film

The Wandering Earth II is indeed much better than the first installment. First of all, the romantic scenes are natural, unforced, and free from artificiality – they feel very real. In particular, Liu Dehua’s portrayal of Tu Hengyu’s excited expression when he reunites with Yaya after 14 years is truly moving. The scene where the veteran astronauts sacrifice themselves to detonate the nuclear bomb using a mechanical fuse is also deeply awe-inspiring. Moreover, unlike the first film, the main crisis in this one isn’t introduced in a random way (the origin of the crisis in the first film is explained at the end, which serves as a plot closure?). The storyline is more coherent and continuous.

In terms of sci-fi special effects, it maintains the same level as the first film. All the vehicles and spaceships have a strong mechanical feel, and their designs are consistent. The space elevator scene at the beginning might be the first time such a concept has been shown on the big screen! The shot of the Arch spaceship crashing is absolutely stunning! There are just two aspects of the special effects I’m less satisfied with: the animation of the nuclear bomb during the lunar detonation lacks detail; it doesn’t look like a real nuclear bomb, but rather just white orbs expanding outward. Is this how a nuclear explosion looks in an oxygen-free environment? I’d love to learn more about this. Also, for the lunar explosion sequence, I feel the wide-angle shot of the initial nuclear explosion wasn’t well-executed. Although the actual explosion range is indeed small, its chain reaction is massive, so the scene of the lunar explosion should have been continued in the same shot. But in reality, the camera zooms in, making the footage of the moon’s collapse seem rather underwhelming.

There are also some flaws. I particularly dislike the “Event XX, countdown XX” text that appears between scenes – it completely ruins the immersive experience of the film. It’s like a reminder that ‘this isn’t real, you’re in a cinema!’ Moreover, it acts like an official spoiler: just as I’m wondering what will happen next, it tells me, destroying all the suspense.

One thing that disappointed me about this film is its failure to explore the ethical issues surrounding mind uploading. These issues offer plenty of room for discussion and are actually one of the main storylines. Questions like whether a uploaded mind counts as a person, the relationship between the uploaded person and the physical body, and what happens if an uploaded mind is replicated – the film doesn’t delve deeply into any of these topics. Also, since the mind uploading technology is already nearly successful, lacking only a suitable operating environment (550C/550W), why weren’t the people who couldn’t enter the underground city turned into uploaded minds? The conflicts between these bodiless uploaded minds and the underground city residents could have been explored in great depth.

I also think the color grading of the film isn’t ideal. Whether it’s due to the theater I went to or not, the contrast feels very low and the brightness is slightly insufficient, as if HDR wasn’t enabled – it’s quite uncomfortable to watch.

Overall, it’s a great film worth watching in an IMAX theater; it lives up to the standard set by the first installment. There’s room for improvement, but it’s still a solid movie.

It’s a shame the ending isn’t as stunning as that of the first film. The scene of the Wandering Earth flying between the pages at the end of the first film was simply breathtaking – it even surpasses Marvel’s opening sequences!
