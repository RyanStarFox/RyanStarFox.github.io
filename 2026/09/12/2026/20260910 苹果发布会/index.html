Ternus时代的第一个One more thing

“Not me, that’s your guy, that’s your opener.”，随着Tim Cook一笑，画面给到John Ternus ，苹果公司这艘大船的舵，交给了Ternus.

其实我对于Ternus的开头不太满意，因为感觉开头Ternus的演讲风格不够好，而且又讲了很多又臭又长Apple Intelligence，siri AI，不知何时可以实现

总结：“欧盟和中国大陆暂时不可用”(一般我们叫环大陆发行)

iPhone 18 Pro

今年的发布会和往年不一样的一点在于，没有发布数字版iPhone，从前年就谣传会把数字版放到春季，今年果真如此

总结一下18 Pro的升级：

  • 可变光圈（说实话感觉提升有限
  • 相机大师模式（早该有了，胶片滤镜不错，与时俱进
  • 更小灵动岛（多放一个app，感知不大
  • 更大散热（号称3倍面积，游戏玩家感知应该很大
  • 60w快充和更长续航（史诗级提升，官方标称18 Pro续航达到17 Pro Max，有了更大电池和C2基带，低信号下性能会更好
  • 性能标准升级

对我来说，除了更长续航（15 Pro比Air还差的续航鞭尸！），没什么非常吸引我的，感觉对于手机的性能已经快电子阳痿了

发布会结束之后在官网机型对比页，还发现一个小细节：国行竟然支持2实体sim/1实体sim+1esim/2esim，这就意味着实际上该机型有2实体sim+esim三个槽位，正式占据电池空间啊！

总结：新机型相比同配置老机型涨价1000，老机型相比老机型涨价800

AirPods

这是我非常期待的

AirPods 5标配降噪，相比于AirPods 4降噪版，降价加量，很不错

但是依旧刀法，得到AirPods Pro同样的上下滑动调节音量和无线充电，要加150元（但是即使如此，也比AirPods 4降噪版降价250元）

在我看来这次的高配版AirPods 5与AirPods 2 Pro一样，有了所有的痛点功能（方便的交互、降噪通透、可用的对话感知），将会是养老产品

这也是我本次发布会唯一想购买的产品

Apple Watch

最便宜的奢侈品，升级了一些健康功能，都是常规升级，没啥好说的

多了一些没什么用的新功能，例如siri recap，官方给出的场景是这样的：

点菜的时候，服务员表演了段报菜名，没听清，怎么办？I人福音！不用让服务员再说一遍了，而是偷偷打开apple watch，siri帮你记了笔记！

看笑了（）

One more thing: iPhone Duo

Cook送给Ternus的传承礼物：

这是一个还没发布就能被抄袭的产品：发布一年半前就被华为抄袭，发布5天前就被小米抄袭的产品（讽刺一下，外网苹果论坛真有人在用这种逻辑骂）

首先夸夸优点：内屏屏下摄像头、还有目前网上广受好评的超浅折痕（虽然存疑，没有人发出过熄屏折痕的照片）、最贵床头钟模式很优雅、分屏模式不错（说你呢iPad，什么时候支持上下分屏！）、折叠动画简直天才

我一直不喜欢折叠屏这个形态，有一些偏见，我认为折叠屏的实用性存疑：

比例问题

折叠屏的最大问题是比例问题，普通折叠屏之后接近正方形，用这个比例看视频、照片和很多软件，有效的面积并没有增大，着实有些鸡肋。这也是华为、小米纷纷推出阔折叠的原因，但是阔折叠则牺牲了折叠状态下的比例，降低了折叠状态下的可用性。（阔折叠把13’的大ipad折成ipad mini更有道理一些）真正解决问题的是三折叠，但是这成本又高得离谱。

苹果没有解决这个不可能三角，而是“抄袭”了华为，使用了阔折叠。解决阔折叠的使用问题，苹果优化了UI，把信息和docker栏放到了右侧。这个真的是only apple can do了，除了苹果，谁还有号召力让软件厂商适配UI！此外，还有一个隐藏的好处在于，左侧展示“正文”区域的大小和比例，实际上和直板机更加接近，这也有利于UI的适配。

但是一看到这个设计，惊叹完“有道理啊”的第二秒，就开始想“那左撇子怎么办？”

这让我想到了两个产品：Apple watch和我的电子书（不对称设计，有侧边按键）。两者都有左右撇子的问题，解决方法是不分上下，倒过来用。电子书倒过来使用体验一模一样，但是Apple watch倒过来只能解决UI，无法解决实体按钮的位置不顺手。

我觉得以苹果关照少数群体的传统，估计肯定会为左撇子适配，但感觉如果也是倒过来用的话，恐怕仍然不够优雅易用。

那一支笔

折叠屏的第二个问题在于其被制造出来是为了能够包括Pad的生态位，但是目前Pad维持其生态位最重要的在于那一支笔。华为、Oppo等厂商确实逐渐引入了那一支手写笔（三星甚至引入了又砍掉了，在我看来不做人事）。但是那一支笔目前的收纳方案仍然无法做到像Pad将笔吸在侧边一样优雅。我本来以为苹果能够拿出一个好方案，结果只是支持了最低端的、没有触感的type c apple pencil。感到也就合格水平。

总结：

  • 价格只是小米的1.5倍，这么看甚至有点划算
  • 想起了被多儿支配的恐惧

这次发布会Craig没整活，差评！

The first “One more thing” of the Ternus era

“Not me, that’s your guy, that’s your opener.” With a smile from Tim Cook, the camera cut to John Ternus—the helmsman of Apple, now taking charge of the company.

To be honest, I wasn’t very satisfied with Ternus’ opening remarks. His presentation style felt underwhelming, and he spent too much time talking about the lengthy and overhyped Apple Intelligence and Siri AI features, whose practical availability remains uncertain.

Summary: “Unavailable in the EU and mainland China for now” (we generally refer to this as a mainland-restricted release).

iPhone 18 Pro

This year’s launch was different from previous years in that Apple did not release a numerical-model iPhone. Rumors from the year before suggested the numerical model would be released in spring, and indeed that’s what happened.

Here’s a summary of the upgrades to the 18 Pro:

  • Variable aperture (to be honest, the improvement feels limited)
  • Camera Master mode (long overdue; the film filters are great and keep up with trends)
  • Smaller Dynamic Island (allowing one extra app to be placed there, but the impact is minimal)
  • Larger cooling system (claimed to be 3 times bigger; gamers should notice a significant difference)
  • 60W fast charging and longer battery life (a game-changing upgrade; Apple claims the 18 Pro’s battery life matches that of the 17 Pro Max. With a larger battery and C2 modem, performance in low-signal areas will also improve)
  • Standard performance upgrades

For me, aside from the longer battery life (the 15 Pro has even worse battery life than the Air model!), nothing else is particularly appealing. It feels like smartphone performance has reached a plateau.

After the launch, I noticed a small detail on the official website’s model comparison page: the Chinese version supports 2 physical SIMs / 1 physical SIM + 1 eSIM / 2 eSIMs. This means the device actually has three slots: 2 physical SIMs + 1 eSIM, which naturally takes up battery space!

Summary: The new model costs ¥1,000 more than the previous-generation model with similar specs, while older models saw an ¥800 price hike compared to their predecessors.

AirPods

This is what I was most looking forward to.

The AirPods 5 comes with noise cancellation as standard. Compared to the AirPods 4 with noise cancellation, it offers lower price and better value, which is great.

However, Apple still applies its usual pricing logic: to get the same volume control via swipe and wireless charging as the AirPods Pro, you need to pay an extra ¥150 (even so, it’s still ¥250 cheaper than the AirPods 4 with noise cancellation).

In my view, this high-end version of the AirPods 5 is just like the AirPods 2 Pro—it includes all the features people have been asking for (convenient interaction, noise cancellation with transparency mode, and usable conversation detection). It will undoubtedly become a go-to product for everyday use.

This is the only product I want to buy from this launch.

Apple Watch

The cheapest luxury item, with some upgraded health features. These are routine updates, nothing particularly noteworthy.

There are also a few useless new features, such as Siri Recap. The official use case is something like this:

You’re ordering food, and the waiter recites the menu quickly—you didn’t catch it all. No problem for introverts! Instead of asking the waiter to repeat it, just sneak a peek at your Apple Watch; Siri took notes for you!

I couldn’t help but laugh at this. ()

One more thing: iPhone Duo

A legacy gift from Cook to Ternus:

This is a product that was copied even before its release: Huawei copied it 1.5 years prior to the launch, and Xiaomi copied it 5 days before release (ironically, some people on foreign Apple forums used this logic to criticize Apple).

Let’s start with the positives: the internal screen features under-display cameras, and the ultra-shallow fold lines are widely praised online (though this is debatable, as no one has shown photos of the screen off with visible fold lines). The elegant “bedside clock” mode and split-screen functionality are great (looking at you, iPad—when will you support vertical split-screen?). The folding animation is simply genius.

I’ve never been a fan of foldable phones and have some biases about their practicality:

Aspect ratio issues

The biggest problem with foldable phones is the aspect ratio. After folding, the screen becomes nearly square. Using this aspect ratio to watch videos, view photos, or use many apps doesn’t increase the usable area much, making it rather useless. This is why Huawei and Xiaomi introduced wider-fold designs, but these sacrifice the usability of the folded state. (A wider-fold design makes more sense if you’re turning a 13-inch iPad into an iPad mini.) The real solution would be a tri-fold design, but the cost is prohibitively high.

Apple didn’t solve this “impossible triangle” but instead “copied” Huawei by opting for a wider-fold design. To address usability issues, Apple optimized the UI, moving notifications and the dock to the right side. This is truly a feat only Apple could achieve—after all, who else has the influence to make software developers adapt to such UI changes? Additionally, a hidden benefit is that the size and aspect ratio of the left-side “content” area closely resemble those of a regular phone, making UI adaptation easier.

But the moment I saw this design and thought, “That makes sense!”, my next thought was, “What about left-handed users?”

This reminds me of two products: the Apple Watch and my e-reader (both have asymmetrical designs with side buttons). Both have issues for left-handed users, and the solution is to use them upside down. An e-reader works fine upside down, but for an Apple Watch, flipping it only fixes the UI—the physical button placement remains inconvenient.

Given Apple’s history of catering to minority groups, they’ll likely provide a solution for left-handed users. But if it still requires using the device upside down, it probably won’t be as elegant or user-friendly.

That stylus

The second issue with foldable phones is that they aim to replace tablets, and the key to maintaining a tablet’s ecosystem lies in that stylus. Huawei, OPPO, and others have gradually introduced styluses (Samsung even introduced one and then removed it—in my opinion, that’s irresponsible). However, the current storage solutions for styluses aren’t as elegant as a tablet’s ability to magnetically attach the pen to its side. I expected Apple to come up with a better solution, but instead, they only supported the cheapest, non-sensitive USB-C Apple Pencil. It’s just passable.

Summary:

  • The price is only 1.5 times that of Xiaomi’s model, so it’s actually quite reasonable.
  • It reminds me of the horror of being dominated by Duo.

Craig didn’t pull any surprises this time—a disappointment!
